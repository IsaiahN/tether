For instance, if i switch a computer switch on and off 50 times in such a way that it ends on the on switch, did the sequence of repeats or the actions i took cause the outcome? no, in that case only one outcome mattered. casuality requires correlation yes, but correlation does not equal causation. and casuality must be tested from several angle to remove what is fluff from what is required to create the conditions for the outcome."

## Unpacked (the five-step rule)

1. **He said:** a count of misses (or of hits) is not evidence by itself. What matters is whether a prediction bears on the conditions that produce an outcome: an event, an unlock, a win. Causation is found by testing from several angles, which separates what is required from what is fluff.
2. **He did not say:** that misses never matter. They matter when they are misses about something that fixes an outcome.
3. **The model he corrects:** my 06:17 framing, which treated "9 of 12 missed" as weighing against the term in itself. That is reading a tally as a verdict.
4. **What replaces it:** a sense is judged by what its predictions bear on. A miss on a prediction that touches no outcome is fluff. A cause is found by difference (change one condition, hold the rest) and by more than one angle, not by how often two things appeared together.
5. **Where it lands:** the c34 read (held despite misses), B2's validity tally, and the release rule for sense clusters (D1/D2).

## WORLD (secondary, each source read)

- **Mill, *A System of Logic*, 1843, via Wikipedia "Mill's methods".**
  - Method of Difference: "If an instance in which the phenomenon under investigation occurs, and an instance in which it does not occur, have every circumstance save one in common ... the circumstance in which alone the two instances differ, is the effect, or cause, or an indispensable part of the cause".
  - Method of Agreement: the one circumstance common to every instance is the cause or effect.
  - Predicts: a cause is isolated by difference, holding everything else equal. Agreement (co-occurrence) is the weaker, first-pass method. Corroborates Isaiah's "tested from several angles".

## VERDICT

1. **A miss is evidence only about what the prediction bears on.** The c34 read is reframed. Not "why was a term kept after 9 of 12 misses", but:
   - (a) what did that term's predictions bear on;
   - (b) did any of its misses touch a condition of an outcome (a level event, an unlock, a win, a residual the agent was working to clear);
   - (c) was the term ever tested by difference: varied, with the rest held, to see whether the outcome followed.
   Misses on predictions that touch no outcome are fluff, and keeping the term through them can be right. Misses on an outcome's conditions are the ones that should release it.
2. **Counts rank; witnesses decide (Fig 9 :60).** B2's validity tally (holds and endings counted) is a filter. It can order what to try. It must not stand in for a causal verdict. The causal verdict comes from:
   - endings (the ground's votes);
   - difference tests: the same situation with one condition changed.
3. **For the sense-cluster release rule (D1/D2):** a sense is released when its own prediction about an outcome-bearing condition misses, not when a raw miss count crosses a line. This sharpens the 18:47 rule ("a cluster is released only when its own prediction misses"): the prediction has to be one that bears on an outcome.

- **Cost if wrong:** the agent keeps a term whose misses did matter, and wastes some steps. Endings correct it.
- **Undoable:** yes; this is a read and a design input, not code yet.
- **Reversed by:** the c34 read showing the kept term's misses DID touch outcome conditions and the agent still held it. That would make it a real hold-too-long bug.
