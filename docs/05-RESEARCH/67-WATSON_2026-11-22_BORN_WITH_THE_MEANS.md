## WORLD (secondary — each source read, corroboration only)
- **Ornithology / life history** — Ehrlich, Dobkin & Wheye, "Precocial and Altricial Young," Stanford (1988): *"People (like other primates, elephants, and antelopes, but unlike rodents and marsupials) are precocial-born with hair, open eyes, and large brains. But our brains and those of parrots, both large at birth, also grow a great deal after birth as a result of large parental investments of food energy."* Also: *"Precocial species trade for this advantage an adult brain that is small in relation to their body size"*; parrots *"evolved their way into the best of both worlds... among the most intelligent of birds."* PREDICTS: **no animal is born with a fully developed brain.** Precocial young are born with a *large* brain — a paid head start — then grow it through provisioning and practice. Isaiah's premise is partly wrong; the correction is the whole lesson. **Corroborates Fig 4.**
- **Evolutionary biology / the "expensive brain"** — van Schaik, Song, Schuppli, Drobniak, Heldstab & Griesser, "Extended parental provisioning and variation in vertebrate brain sizes," PLoS Biology 21(2):e3002016 (2023): *"Large brains... become fully functional well after their growth is completed. Consequently, young of most larger-brained endotherms should not be able to independently support the growth and development of their own brains. This paradox is solved if the evolution of extended parental provisioning facilitated brain size evolution."* And: *"brains are unusual organs in that they must acquire their cognitive and motor functions through practice and learning and therefore perform poorly before they are fully grown and differentiated."* PREDICTS: the bill for a capable brain is paid externally, before and during development, in a different currency (energy); capability then comes from practice, not from the brain arriving finished. **Corroborates Fig 4 + Fig 11.**
- **Comparative cognition / development** — Barton & Capellini (Proc R Soc B, 2011, via the 2023 review): *"brain growth is primarily related to the duration and rate of maternal investment."* The 2023 review: *"proper brain development requires play, which is often quite vigorous and therefore energetically expensive... primate species with more postnatal brain growth... play more."* PREDICTS: the head start is a *measurable investment*, and play is the paid practice that turns structure into skill — corroborating the project's own finding that games are how the agent pays the bill and acquires tacit knowledge (WATSON 31). **Corroborates Fig 3 step 5.**

## VERDICT (from the figures)
1. **The catch-22 is false as stated.** The copyright restriction applies to RECORDINGS (walkthrough text), and Fig 4 forbids recordings crossing down precisely — "a head start, never a recording." What must be frontloaded is a MEANS: vocabulary, instruments, operators, grammar, the ability to iterate and compose. Those carry no copyright and are already shipped (~2,700 atoms, nine sensors, the bond grammar). The walkthrough harvest was one *data-lite, optional, correctly paused* head-start source — never the intelligence itself. Removing it removes a convenience, not the mechanism.
2. **Intelligence comes from the loop, not the corpus.** Fig 6: instruments are improved from a worse reading already returning something; the agent's residuals are those readings. Fig 3 step 5: verify by the ground, then carry the method. This is the architecture's standing claim — the agent gets smarter through recursion against a ground nobody authored. A model that needed the traces to be intelligent would be a lookup table, which is the failure the project exists to refuse.
3. **The animal analogue, correctly read, says the same thing.** No animal is born with a finished brain. Precocial ones are born with a *large, paid-for head start* and become capable through provisioning + play. "Born with the means, then earn the skill" — which is exactly Fig 4's membrane. There is no training-free intelligence; there is intelligence whose costly structure was paid before contact (inheritance/frontload) and whose skill is earned cheaply in a protected habitat. We have one agent and a deadline, so the bill is paid the only other way open: inheritance.
4. **Past ARC-3 → real problems = Fig 11.** Capability is agent-and-habitat. Move the habitat to real problems and read the residuals the agent cannot yet resolve; that is the next method, and the ground — not a proxy — judges it. This is a habitat change, not a training-data change.

**Carry the method (step 7):** when a capability looks blocked by missing data, first check whether what is missing is a MEANS (frontloadable, legal, buildable) or a RECORDING (copyrighted, forbidden) — the two are separated by Fig 4's membrane, and confusing them manufactures a catch-22. The animal-development frame (paid head start + practice in a protected habitat) is the reusable outward template for this whole class.

**Cost if wrong:** treating recordings as the missing means would waste the deadline chasing unshippable data and encode answers if obtained — the unforgivable failure. **Undoable:** yes — the frontload is vocabulary and grammar, editable without touching the loop. **Reversed by:** a per-game ground result showing the agent's failures are due to a *vocabulary/instrument it does not hold* (a means gap), not a contact/reach gap — then, and only then, an entry-rule frontload is licensed.

## To Isaiah, in plain words
There is no catch-22, because the thing copyright blocks is the one thing the architecture never needed. Walkthroughs are recordings, and the figures say recordings don't cross down — only head starts do. What has to be frontloaded is the *means* to think (vocabulary, instruments, operators), which nobody can copyright and which is already in the agent. Elephants aren't born with finished brains either — they're born with a big head start that someone else paid for, then get capable through care and play. That's the same rule: pay for the structure up front, earn the skill through contact. The agent already has the structure; the reason it hasn't generalised is the measured break (it doesn't clear games / reach board 2), not a shortage of training text. Moving to real problems is a habitat change — put the agent in real habitats and let the ground show what it can't yet resolve. I have not changed any code; this is the derivation.
