PART 1 — WHAT ANALYSIS PARALYSIS IS, IN THE AGENT'S TERMS
===============================================================================

RESIDUAL (effect terms, Fig 9 :30-31 "describe the effect rather than the cause"):
"a single step consumed 53 minutes and 43,102 tied conditions; deliberation kept producing
candidates but the action never came." Not slowness — a step whose SEARCH never converted to
a DECISION.

DOMAIN CENSUS:
- R: one decision (arity: candidate set → chosen action; symmetry: more analysis can help or
  harm; scale: one step).
- domains: (a) the search — actors candidates/bits, currency description length, anchor Fig 5;
  (b) the decision — actors the action, currency actions-to-find-out, anchor Fig 12/ground;
  (c) the clock — actors the step's deadline, currency wall, anchor the harness (Fig 10).
- union: "analysed enough" ∩ "can afford to test" is one compound, two currencies (Fig 12).
- order (Fig 3): description (bits) → reachability (actions) → decision.

TETHER (primary, verbatim):
- Fig 12 :125-126 (figures_text 824-826): "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do
  not add. Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in
  different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them and will not notice
  the shortfall. It proposes arrangements it cannot afford to test, and the budget is what
  decides how much of the space is reached at all." → ANALYSIS PARALYSIS IS A ONE-CURRENCY
  FAILURE: the search prices bits (43,102 tied candidates all cheap to describe) and never
  prices the ACTIONS needed to settle them. The agent proposes what it cannot afford to test.
- Fig 6 :49-50 "more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? ... Fails by spending
  budget on a region already swept." → the 43,102 ties are one region swept repeatedly.
- Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand
  you a verdict." → a budget cut must be a filter (what was unreached), never "this is false."
- Fig 5 :16 "mint if the description length pays"; Fig 13 "a wrong cut removes the answer and
  speeds up / keep every cut ranked and reversible" → cuts must be ranked/reversible.
- Fig 11 :39-40 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat ... an improvement that does not
  change contact changes nothing." → 53 min of internal search changed no contact; it is the
  definition of an improvement that changes nothing.

WORLD (secondary — witnesses only, never overrule):
- Herbert Simon, bounded rationality and SATISFICING: search until an option clears "good
  enough" then stop (Mark Shrime summary, markshrime.com, 2026). "Deliberation has stopped
  producing returns. It can feel like spinning ... additional analysis no longer winnows out
  options. In fact, it introduces new ones." → PREDICTS a stopping rule on marginal yield,
  not on a fixed time. Corroborates Fig 6 :49-50.
- Iyengar & Lepper 2000 (jam study): 24 options → fewer purchases than 6 → option overload
  suppresses decision. → PREDICTS cap the candidate set, not the clock. Corroborates Fig 6.
- Colin Powell rule (ISACA 2024): act at 40-70% of needed information. → PREDICTS a sufficiency
  threshold. Corroborates satisficing.
- Pezzulo/Cisek affordance landscape (used before): affordances are acted on in a competition,
  not exhaustively evaluated. → PREDICTS the action competes before analysis completes.
- Frontiers 2024 satisficing in active perception: humans DROP less informative features under
  time pressure — they shed the analysis, not the decision. → PREDICTS prune candidates, not
  stall the action.

VERDICT (from the figures, triangulated Fig 12 + Fig 6 + Fig 9 + Fig 11):
Analysis paralysis is NOT a clock problem; it is the failure to read the SECOND currency. The
agent's search prices description bits and never asks what a candidate would cost to SETTLE.
Raise the time cap and it keeps proposing unaffordable arrangements for longer — Fig 6 :49-50.
The cure is to price the action-cost of a candidate as a first-class reading and STOP searching
when the candidate set stops yielding new reachable distinctions (satisficing), keeping every
cut ranked and reversible. The deadline is only the outer safety net, not the decision rule.

===============================================================================
PART 2 — WHEN TO THINK LONGER, AND WHEN NOT
===============================================================================

RESIDUAL: "the agent spent 53 min on one step with 43,102 ties — was that the step that
DESERVED 53 min, or one that should have been settled in one action?"

TETHER (primary):
- Fig 12 :127-129 "A long chain of cheap parts is expensive to describe and may cost one action
  to refute. A short arrangement over a costly operand is cheap to state and may take a whole
  budget to settle. Neither figure predicts the other, so both are read or the choice is made
  blind." → THIS IS THE DECISION RULE. Think longer exactly when the ACTION-cost is high: a
  short arrangement over a costly operand (hard-to-observe outcome), where one more test can
  discriminate. Think briefly when description is long but a single action refutes it.
- Fig 2 :92 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." → spend more when a
  wrong decision is costly AND frames agree for the wrong reason.
- Fig 9 :41-42 "narrowing gets you close, the ground decides"; :5 "disagreements that do not
  shrink with effort ... are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it."
  → when narrowing stops shrinking the set, do NOT think longer; split or leave.
- Fig 10 (the end-reserve): the last portion of the wall is reserved for the end path; thinking
  must never eat the settlement.
- Fig 1 (per-slot): a residual with no slot is not a thinking occasion.

WORLD: satisficing under cost-of-error (Simon); "act at 40-70%" (Powell); time-pressure studies
show humans shed FEATURES when the decision is cheap, keep them when it is costly (Frontiers
2024, Oh et al. 2016). → PREDICTS effort tracks cost-of-being-wrong, not a fixed clock.

VERDICT: think longer ONLY when (a) the action-cost of settling is high and a further test can
discriminate, or (b) the cost of being wrong is high and independence must be bought. Think
briefly when a single action can refute an elaborate description, when the candidate set has
stopped shrinking (split instead), or when the end-reserve is approaching. Never let thinking
consume the settlement — Fig 9 :60 filters never hand verdicts.

===============================================================================
PART 3 — HOW TO SHORTCUT / SPEED UP DECISION MAKING
===============================================================================

RESIDUAL: "43,102 tied conditions in one step — the tie-break and the search must be cheaper."

TETHER (primary):
- Fig 9 :29 "matching is a one-pass check, not a search." → replace the sweep with a one-pass
  shape match (the retrieval key: type/arity/invariance/effect-shape).
- Fig 6 :49-50 "Distribute the search, and keep the searchers independent" + :53 "searchers
  drawing on the same evidence are one searcher wearing many names." → parallel independent
  probes, not more candidates.
- Fig 5 :16 mint when the description length pays; Fig 2 :92 buy independence in proportion
  to cost → the STOP rule is marginal yield: when more candidates stop shrinking the residual,
  stop and test the best.
- Fig 13 / operators "keep every cut ranked and reversible" → the shortcut is a RANK + a cut,
  never a deletion.
- Fig 4 "re-deriving it is a lookup and the actions were spent once" → reuse settled results
  (the caching/hoisting/TOP-K already built) rather than recompute.

WORLD: satisficing "good enough then stop"; one-pass recognition (the agent's AKAZE shape match);
TOP-K search engines (built as TETHER_SERP).

VERDICT: the shortcuts are (1) one-pass matching instead of exhaustive sweep, (2) an explicit
action-cost reading per candidate so an unaffordable-to-test arrangement is ranked last, (3) a
marginal-yield STOP so the search ends when the residual stops shrinking, (4) keep all cuts ranked
and reversible, (5) reuse via the existing lookup/memo infrastructure. None of these is a
shorter clock; the clock remains only the outer guard.

ASSEMBLY — WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE AGENT (and what NOT to do)
===============================================================================
The 1800 s proving run currently going is treating the symptom: it gives the one-currency
failure more time to sweep. The FIX, derived above, is to make the agent read the second
currency (actions-to-settle) at proposal time, cap the candidate set by marginal yield, and
stop when narrowing stops shrinking — then act. Keep the deadline as the outer safety net and
the supervisor's memory ceiling as the runaway guard; never remove either (a 53-min step is
the failure, not a budget to accommodate).

CONCRETE next steps (each needs its own WATSON at the write site before code):
- A proposal-time "action-cost" reading: for the top candidates, estimate how many actions it
  would take to settle them (from the agent's own delta table); rank by (bits, action-cost),
  not bits alone. Must-fail: a cheap-to-describe but expensive-to-test candidate loses to a
  slightly longer but cheap-to-test one.
- A marginal-yield stop in the mint/route search: when the last N candidates produced no new
  reachable distinction, stop and test the best. Reversible, behind an arm.
- Measure on a real board: does the 53-min step disappear with the action-cost reading, and
  does the chain reach further? Per game, never pooled.

cost if wrong: a candidate is stopped early that would have settled — mitigated by keeping cuts
ranked/reversible and by raising the stop threshold only with a measured yield. undoable: yes,
each behind its own arm with an OFF identity row. reversed by: a board where the long sweep
clears where the short one did not (then the action-cost estimate was wrong).

## Analysis paralysis — measured, with the gate ON (2026-11-21)

Isaiah's question: "humans and animals unless under restraint or true duress never take 53 mins
to move ... WATSON how to overcome analysis paralysis, when to think longer, how to shortcut."

and never the actions needed to settle), cured by an action-cost reading, a marginal-yield stop,
one-pass matching, and reversible cuts. It recomputes the deadline as the outer guard only.

DECISION: do NOT raise the time cap as a fix for the 53-min step — that was treating a stale

marginal-yield stop. No ARC-game tuning, no new thresholds, no check weakened.
