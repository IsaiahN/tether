## Unpacked (the five-step rule)

1. **He said:**
   - The question "drop or keep?" was the wrong one. A held claim that meets contrary evidence was never a rule; it was a guess or hypothesis.
   - The contrary evidence says there are conditions at play the agent does not yet know.
   - The response is to adjust the claim and find those conditions by testing, inference and observation, so the wanted outcome can be made to happen: by the agent's own act, or by waiting for a window when the conditions are outside its control.
   - Claims differ in STRENGTH, from hunch to law. Responses differ from reflex to strategy.
   - LAW, for this agent, is only the Tether itself.
   - It is not a hierarchy of importance: one of the Tether's structures, a range of strength.
2. **He did not say** that a contradicted claim is deleted, or that strength means importance.
3. **The model he corrects:** mine. I framed it as "drop the rule or keep it", which treats a claim's standing as binary and calls a one-cycle guess a "rule".
4. **What replaces it:**
   - a claim sits somewhere on a range of strength;
   - a contradiction moves it down that range and opens a search for the hidden condition;
   - the condition found is a new, stronger, conditioned claim;
   - the response a claim can support runs from slow (strategy) to fast (reflex) as its strength rises.
5. **Where it lands:**
   - D2 (when a claim is held or released) is reframed: not release, but weaken plus a condition search;

## WORLD (secondary, each source read)

- **Philosophy of science: a failed prediction implicates the conditions.** Duhem-Quine thesis (Wikipedia): "an empirical test of the hypothesis requires one or more background assumptions", so "the blame can be placed on one of the background beliefs or 'auxiliary' hypotheses."
  -> Predicts: a contradiction says some condition is wrong or missing, not necessarily the core claim. Isaiah's "conditions at play". Corroborates.
- **Hypothesis, theory and law are different KINDS, not rungs.** Baird (West Texas A&M, "Science Questions with Surprising Answers"): "A hypothesis is a guessed outcome of a particular experiment"; "A physical law is a single statement that the universe obeys"; "A scientific theory is a collection of laws, principles, concepts, and facts united together." He does not rank law above theory: laws are parts of theories.
  -> Corrects the list's order (Isaiah's "not exactly"): theory is broader in scope, not weaker, than a rule or law. So strength and scope are two different things. The spectrum is strength; scope (one regularity vs a framework that unites many) is a separate axis. Partly conflicts with the list's order. Per the WATSON rule the figures stand: Fig 13's spectrum orders strength only, and scope is not ranked on it.
- **Infants: a contradiction drives targeted exploration.** Stahl & Feigenson 2015 (*Science* 348:91-94): infants "learned more effectively about objects that committed violations", "explored those objects more", with "hypothesis-testing behaviors that reflected the particular kind of violation seen". (An object that passed through a wall was banged; one that hovered was dropped.)
  -> Predicts: a contradiction should start a curious, aimed test of the specific violation, not a deletion. That ties straight to play. Corroborates.
- **Human factors: response modes by familiarity.** Rasmussen 1983, "Skills, Rules, and Knowledge":
  - skill-based is "smooth, automated, and highly integrated";
  - rule-based uses "a procedure you might have 'stored', derived empirically";
  - "In unfamiliar situations when proven rules are not available, behavior may be goal-controlled", meaning knowledge-based: goal, plan, test.
  -> Predicts Isaiah's response range (reflex, reaction, action, strategy), and that a broken rule pushes behaviour up to planning and testing. Corroborates.

1. **The structure is a spectrum of strength (Fig 13), not a ladder.** More and less can be said, with no rank of importance. Strong claims are spent as fast responses: that is the gradient between the two lists. Scope (one regularity vs a framework that unites many) is a separate axis, not a position on this spectrum.
