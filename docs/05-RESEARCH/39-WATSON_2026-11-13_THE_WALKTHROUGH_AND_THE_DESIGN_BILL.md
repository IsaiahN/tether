# WATSON 2026-11-13: the walkthrough and the design bill

**The question.** Inside idea-making, the agent reads the board twice: once for what it is in contact with, and once for whether an idea could reach its target. Since SHIP 1 those reads carry the idea-making label but are not charged to the thinking budget. Should they be charged?

**Isaiah's answer, verbatim (2026-11-13 00:10).** Three key sentences:
- "The trip back to the walk is apart of the design, every thing after the lookup is apart of the design up until the deal is presented and options laid out."
- "what makes the looking expensive is the inexperienced agent. To the senior the looking is cheap and saves time and money."
- "For the novice the design is the most valuable part because he can make mistakes on paper instead of with materials."

His full message also covers the following:
- **Measure twice, cut once.** A confirming read taken at a different time, or from a different frame with a different instrument, rules out a miscalibrated instrument.
- **The walkthrough comes first, and it gates everything after it.** It primes the contractor and gives him the lay of the land: skills, tools, effort, time, difficulty, and above all whether the job is possible. If this tier does not pass muster, nothing proceeds. Scope starts here, fast for an expert who has paid the bill before.
- **Discovery is measuring again with a finer ruler.** It is taxing work that fills in the lower tiers of the HTN: material grades, quantities with a buffer, equipment, order of operations, and questions asked while taking notes. The result is one or a few estimates and a timeline.
- **Fast is not cheap.** You pay for the expertise that lowers the risk of failure and spots the real cause, sometimes one the customer misstated.
- **A novice can borrow expertise.** Advice from others shortens his learning curve.
- **The expert's walkthrough narrows the causality chain;** the novice can only pay that bill down in design.

## World (secondary, each source read)

- **Chess perception.** Chase & Simon, *Perception in chess*, Cognitive Psychology, 1973.
  - After a brief look at a real game position, masters replaced about 25 pieces "with almost perfect accuracy (about 93% correct)"; novices "could recall only about eight pieces."
  - On random positions, "all players, from master to novice, recalled only about three or four pieces."
  - The master's look is cheap and rich only where his stored patterns apply. **Corroborates.**
- **Fireground commanders.** Klein's recognition-primed decision model: "people can use experience to generate a plausible option as the first one they consider."
  - This is the expert walkthrough that skips trial and error. **Corroborates.**
- **Rats at a choice point.** Gardner et al., *Neurobiology of Learning and Memory* 131, 2016: vicarious trial and error is "the tendency for rats to pause at a choice point and look back and forth toward potential destinations," and it "decreased with continued task experience."
  - In animals, looking is expensive for the novice and falls with experience. **Corroborates.**
- **Medicine.** Hampton et al., *BMJ*, 1975: in 66 of 80 medical outpatients, the history ("the walkthrough") reached the final diagnosis. The examination was useful in 7, and tests changed the diagnosis in 7.
  - The cheap first look decides most cases, and finer instruments refine them. **Corroborates.**
- **Remodelling.** Pro Remodeler, *trade walkthroughs*: they are done "to minimize surprises and change orders after deconstruction"; errors found then "can easily be changed" because "it is only on paper."
  - The article warns: "A contract without a trade walkthrough can be a disaster, for either party involved." **Corroborates.**
- **Design effort and the cost of change.** The MacLeamy curve (traced to Paulson 1976 and Boehm 1976): a project "becomes more difficult to change the more developed it becomes," so effort is frontloaded.
  - A critic notes it "should be treated as a diagram rather than a statement of fact." **Corroborates, with that caveat.**
- **What to avoid: trusting a look outside its range.** Kahneman & Klein (McKinsey Quarterly, 2010) on when to trust expert intuition:
  - It needs "a certain predictability," and decision makers need "a chance to get feedback on their judgments."
  - Kahneman trusts it for "something that they have dealt with a lot in the past."
  - Their low-cost guard is the premortem: assume the project failed and write down why.
  - The senior's cheap look is valid only in a regular environment with feedback. On a new kind of board he is a novice again (VTE also rises when strategies conflict). **Corroborates and bounds the claim.**
- **What to avoid: the inside view.** Flyvbjerg, *Project Management Journal* 37(3), 2006: reference-class forecasting, "a view that bypasses human biases by cutting directly to outcomes."
  - This is the novice's borrowed expertise: price the job from how similar jobs went, not from his own plan. **Corroborates Isaiah's "borrow expertise from learning from others advice."**
- **How to become senior.** Ericsson, Krampe & Tesch-Römer, *Psychological Review* 100(3), 1993: expert performance is "the end result of individuals' prolonged efforts to improve performance," through "effortful activities (deliberate practice) designed to optimize improvement."
  - Seniority is paid for in deliberate, measured attempts, not in time served. **Corroborates.**

## Verdict (from the figures)

1. **Charge the two reads inside idea-making to idea-making** (Isaiah's ruling; Fig 12 :125-127, Fig 9 :45).
   - A read made in order to design is part of design's price.
   - The free look stays free: perceiving, accepting, settling and repeating.
2. **Let experience make the look cheap, rather than making the look free.** The agent already has the means:
   - the index and memos it carries;
   - recognised kinds;
   - from tonight, the learned search order carried across generations.
   - A charged read that hits a known kind should cost little. A charged read on a new kind costs what a novice pays (Fig 4 :24).
3. **The possibility check belongs to the walkthrough, before design is charged** (Fig 13 :133, Fig 9 :45 "only in that order").
   - Today the reach check runs inside idea-making. Moving it ahead of design would stop impossible jobs before any design is paid for.
   - This is a proposal for Isaiah, not built.
4. **Measure twice, with a second instrument** (Fig 2 :31, :42), wherever being wrong is costly.

**Cost if wrong:**
- Charging the reads gives the agent fewer ideas on crowded boards.
- Leaving them free lets real work hide under "looking".

**Undoable:** yes. It goes behind its own switch, default decided by the measurement.

**Reversed by:**
- the measurement showing the reads cost nothing anywhere (then the choice does not matter), or
- a 25-game reading where charging costs levels without cutting wasted ideas.

**The measurement now has a sharper job:** not "should it be charged" (decided) but "what does it cost a novice versus an experienced agent":
- the read's cost on a kind's first sighting versus later sightings;
- across a generation boundary.
