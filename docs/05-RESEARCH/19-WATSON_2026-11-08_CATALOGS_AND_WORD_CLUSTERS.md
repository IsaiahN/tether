## VERDICT

**NAMES (Isaiah's, adopted):**
- **CATALOG:** the organization each kind has. Every operator, and every kind in the NSM grammar, has a catalog.
- **CLUSTER (word cluster):** one group in a catalog. It is one sense of that kind in one context, with:
  - a HEADWORD, the label (Roget);
  - its COLLOCATES, what co-occurs with it and paid;
  - its OUTCOME predictions.
  - Inside a cluster, members sit in circles by contact level, closest first. Its importance is its CENTRALITY (S4).
- No collision: "catalog" and "cluster" do not appear in the figures, and the library's ATTRIBUTE_CLUSTERS already uses "cluster" in this same sense.

**EXAMPLE: the catalog for "−".** The core, every cluster's shared part, is "A with B removed or absent" (Operators Table :73).
- Board cluster: an object leaves or disappears; collocates are contact and position. Outcome: fewer objects.
- Count cluster: fewer, LESS (the NSM word, Isaiah 11-04); collocates are number attributes. Outcome: a smaller count.
- Ledger cluster: debt, cost, spend; collocates are a budget or counter. Outcome: a resource falls.
- Set cluster: exclude, filter; collocates are kinds and membership. Outcome: a smaller set.
These are different terms by what "−" is bonded to (Fig 13 :75-76), yet linked through the shared core. That is Isaiah's "linked but separate islands".

**HOW IT WORKS IN THE AGENT (D1 renamed: CLUSTERS):**
1. The cues in contact are matched against the collocates of each cluster in the relevant catalogs. The best-matching cluster is the FILTER: only its senses and outcome predictions are offered (Yarowsky's one sense per collocation).
2. Within a game, a chosen cluster holds until a prediction gap fires (one sense per discourse; the 11-02 tier rule). That is cheap and consistent.
3. Clusters GROW by paid use: a collocate joins after a critical mass of paid endings, and is blocked if it adds nothing (D2).
4. Clusters SPLIT when one kind's outcomes in one context stop matching its outcomes elsewhere. The question "is not one question and must be split" (Fig 13 :136). Splitting is how "−" in a ledger became its own cluster.
5. Clusters MERGE when two contexts produce the same outcomes (S2's shared-outcome concepts).
6. The catalog, its clusters, and their collocates are FILE FACETS in S1's index. So the cue round-trip test checks that a cue reaches the right CLUSTER, not just the right kind.

- **Cost if wrong:** cluster boundaries may be drawn too fine or too coarse at first. Split and merge are driven by endings, so they self-correct. Reversible.
- **Reversed by:** the retest showing cluster selection picking a wrong sense more often than the unfiltered kind would. That would mean collocates are mis-weighted.
