## The split this sets

| Layer | Owns | Judged by |
|---|---|---|
| Reasoning ("conscious") | The intention, its progress, and the choice of route | Progress = the intention's gap shrinking |
| Interface ("subconscious") | Turning an intention into actions | Did this realisation close the intention's gap, here? |

The reasoning layer never reads which action got credit for which slot. That is the interface's private business.

## OUTWARD WITNESSES (WATSON)

1. **Control the goal, not the movement.**
   - Powers' Perceptual Control Theory (1973, *Behavior: The Control of Perception*): behaviour varies in whatever way keeps a perceived variable at its reference. The organism controls the outcome, and the actions are whatever does that. ([Wikipedia: PCT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perceptual_control_theory))
   - Caveat: PCT is a minority school. It is cited here for the shape of the idea, not as settled science.
2. **The motor system corrects only what matters to the task.**
   - Todorov & Jordan's minimal intervention principle (NIPS 2002): an optimal feedback controller corrects deviations that interfere with the task goal and leaves task-irrelevant variation alone. ([paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2002/hash/8c5f6ecd29a0eb234459190ca51c16dd-Abstract.html))
   - That is Isaiah's "the subconscious cares how": its error signal is defined in task space, not limb by limb.
3. **Motor equivalence.** The same goal is reached by different effectors and different movements (Lashley; Bernstein, the degrees-of-freedom problem). ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degrees_of_freedom_problem); learning that transfers across effectors: [Kelso & Zanone 2002](https://ccs.fau.edu/hbblab/pdfs/ordered/2002-kelso-zanone-learning-across-effectors.pdf))
4. **Progress is monitored as a rate.**
   - Carver & Scheier (1990, *Psychological Review*): a second loop watches the RATE at which the discrepancy is shrinking. Affect reports whether that rate is good enough.
   - Discrepancy that is not being reduced is the signal, and the actions merely occurring is not. ([overview, BU AMP lab](https://sites.bu.edu/amplab/files/2024/06/Goal-pursuit.pdf))
5. **Let go of the unattainable, and re-engage.**
   - Wrosch, Scheier, Miller, Schulz & Carver (2003, *PSPB* 29): when a goal is unattainable, disengaging AND re-engaging with another goal goes with better outcomes than persisting. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15018681/))
   - Its agent analogue: refuse the route or the intention at budget, then pick the next intention.
6. **Subtract what you cause or what happens anyway.**
   - von Holst & Mittelstaedt's reafference principle (1950): the nervous system predicts the sensory result of its own command (efference copy) and subtracts it, and the remainder is the world. ([Wikipedia: efference copy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efference_copy))
   - This belongs INSIDE the interface. It is how the subconscious learns its mechanics without being fooled by its own clock.
7. **The goal is held fixed while the means adapt to obstacles.**
   - Gergely, Nádasdy, Csibra & Bíró (1995, *Cognition*): 12-month-olds expect an agent to jump an obstacle to reach its goal, and to take the direct path once the obstacle is gone. Goal constant, path chosen by the constraints. ([Gergely & Csibra 1997](https://energy.ceu.edu/sites/default/files/publications/gergelycsibra1997.pdf); related: [Liu & Spelke 2016](https://www.harvardlds.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/Liu-Spelke_Expect-Agents-Minimize-Cost_2016.pdf))
8. **Detours.**
   - Reaching a goal often means first moving away from it. The detour paradigm is a standard test across species ([Kabadayi et al. 2018, *Animal Cognition*](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s10071-017-1152-0)).
   - Caveat: Tolman's classic shortcut result (1948) replicates poorly by a recent meta-analysis ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12766676/)), so "cognitive maps" are not leaned on here.
9. **Subgoals when the direct operator does not apply.**
   - Newell & Simon's means-ends analysis (GPS, *Human Problem Solving* 1972): reduce the difference. If no operator applies, set a subgoal that makes one apply. ([overview](https://www.instructionaldesign.org/?p=145))
   - This is the "winding path of other things".
10. **Impasse means changing the representation, not trying harder.**
    - Ohlsson's representational change theory; Knoblich et al. (1999): an impasse is broken by relaxing a constraint or decomposing a chunk. ([Knoblich et al. 1999](https://www.kognition.uni-koeln.de/literature/Knoblich_etal_1999.pdf))
    - This is the escape room: when every route the agent can see is refused, the next move is to re-describe the situation, not to press again.
11. **Measures that become targets.** Goodhart (1975); reward hacking (Amodei et al. 2016, [arXiv:1606.06565](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.06565)). The ls20 clock want is a small, honest instance of this.

## THE COMMON CASES

What a person does in each case, what the agent needs at each layer, and what we have seen. A "route" is one realisation, or one adopted subgoal, serving the intention.

| # | Case | What a person does | Reasoning layer needs | Interface layer needs | Seen |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Direct route works | Keeps going while the distance shrinks | Gap shrinks, so continue | Nothing new | Normal play |
| 2 | Blocked (a wall) | Stops pushing within a try or two and goes around | No shrink over the trend's repeat count: refuse THIS route here, keep the intention, ask for another route | Learn "this does nothing here", net of reafference | ls20 c24-31. Perception learned "doesn't move" in 2 presses (R7); reasoning kept pressing |
| 3 | Detour or prerequisite (a key, a button, a door) | Temporarily goes away from the goal to do something else | Adopt a SUBGOAL. While it is held, progress is the subgoal's gap. | 4 | Intention impossible this way (an escape room, or two clocks that never meet) | Tries the routes it can see, then re-describes the room or sets the goal aside | Every route refused at budget: record "unreachable at budget" (never "impossible"), keep the refusal, re-engage another intention (Wrosch) | Nothing | ls20 o21.age = o15.age; also a time bar that "looks like a target" |

## WHAT THIS CHANGES IN THE DESIGN (supersedes P1-P5 where they differ)

**Reasoning layer: intention and progress.**
- **R-A. Progress is a subtraction.** It is the change in the intention's raw gap, read every step (was P2). Never normalised by anything the agent's vocabulary grows, and never read from the interface's credit.
- **R-B. Refuse the ROUTE, not the intention.** No shrink over the trend's existing repeat count (MIN_REPEAT, already in the code), or any "farther" reading, refuses THAT route in THAT context. The refusal is kept as learned (Fig 6). The intention stays and asks for another route. This was P3, now aimed at the route.
- **R-C. Subgoals are routes.** While an adopted subgoal is held, the route is judged on the subgoal's gap, and the parent may grow. This prevents R-B from killing detours (case 3).
- **R-D. Give up at budget, and re-engage.** When the routes the agent can reach at its budget are all refused, the intention is recorded "unreachable at budget" in that context, and the next intention is taken up. The refusals carry across tries in that context. That is the "eliminating what it already tried" Isaiah's ls20 test asks for.
- **R-E. Delayed effects.** A route whose bet predicts a later effect is refused only after the predicted window passes (case 6).

**Interface layer: the subconscious.**
- **I-A. One signal comes down from above:** did this realisation close the intention's gap, here? That is how the interface learns which action serves which intention in which context (task-space error, Todorov).
- **I-B. Reafference inside.** Whatever the interface keeps about slot changes is net of what happens anyway (own clocks, self-movers). This is where P1's credit half and P4's shared "moves by itself" reading now live.
- **I-C. Use the current prediction first.** When choosing a realisation, prefer what perception predicts for this press here over the pooled past. This is P5, now an interface matter.
- **The reasoning layer never reads I-B's table** as evidence of progress (Isaiah: "None of that cares which leg gets credit").

**ISAIAH'S CAVEAT, 19:19 (verbatim):**

> "Our interface mainly just takes the general intent and directional info, so that would still belong to the agent, like: move this here, or I want to go there, or I want this interaction. The interface mainly translates and works out how that logic translates, just like we have it. But the agent will need to know when that's not working. Like be able to almost steer or course-correct, like if the interface interpreted wrongly?"

- **R-F. Steering: the agent checks the translation at the level of its own intent.**
  - The intent already carries its direction ("move this here", "go there", "this interaction"), so the agent knows what it EXPECTS to see. That expectation is the efference copy, held at the intention level, not the limb level. The agent's existing per-slot bet on the intent's subject is already this.
  - After each realised press, the agent compares the intent's subject against that expectation. It never compares against which action was used. There are four readings:
    1. **On course:** the subject moved the intended way, and the gap shrank. Keep going.
    2. **Nothing happened:** the subject did not change. Tell the interface "not this way, here", and the interface offers its next realisation. This is the wall case, and ls20 c24-31.
    3. **Wrong way:** the subject moved, but away from the target. The interface misinterpreted, so correct at once: tell the interface "that realisation does the opposite, here". The agent does not reason about which button; it just re-asks with the error. This is the toggle and undo case.
    4. **Wrong thing:** something else moved, not the subject. The intent was misread (wrong object or wrong interaction). Re-state the intent more specifically, for example name the subject or the target, and re-ask.
  - **Interface misread, or world blocked?** If several realisations in this context all read "nothing happened" or "wrong way", the problem is the route, not the translation, and R-B refuses the route. If a different realisation of the same intent then works, the earlier failures were the translation. The interface learns that through I-A, and the agent never needs to know which button it was.
  - Like steering a car: you watch the car drift, not the steering column, and turn more or less until the drift stops.
  - **What already exists:** the per-slot bets give the expectation. Realisation.evidence (the record addition now in testing) records which realisation was used and what it expected. What is MISSING is the intent-level comparison that feeds the choice and the interface. That is R-F.
  - **Test:** a game-free world where one action's direction is inverted (the interface's first guess is wrong). It is a must-fail that today's code repeats the wrong press. With R-F, the correction comes after the first wrong-way reading.

**Unchanged:** P1's target ban stays withdrawn. Any intention is a legitimate guess.

**Order (Fig 3):** R-A and R-B first, because they are link 3 ("what would count as progress"). Then R-C, R-D and R-E, then I-A to I-C.
- The across-tries ls20 test comes only after R-A to R-D. Otherwise it reads below the break.
- The speed work is separate and still needed.

**Tests (wiring, Fig 11 :70):** one game-free world per case, 1-10. Each is a must-fail with occasions > 0 that fails on today's code where today's code is wrong:
- cases 2 and 4 fail today;
- case 7 fails today;
- case 3 must NOT be broken by R-B.

**Capability** is read only on real games afterwards, never ls20 alone, and nothing is tuned to any game.

## CAVEATS

- PCT is a minority framework.
- Tolman's shortcut evidence is weak.
- Carver-Scheier's rate-affect link is a model, not a law.

They are used for shape, and the design rests on the figures. Cases 6 and 8 are not yet observed in our runs, so their rules are derived and unmeasured.
