## Unpacked (the five-step rule)

1. **He said:**
   - A succession of linked games is itself a game, one tier up: a tournament. The same players play every game in it.
   - Whether the games are taken one at a time with pauses (a book series read as it is written), or all at once (the finished anthology), it is the SAME reader. What differs is what is learned: pausing gives time to absorb and contextualize; reading straight through lets the story guide you.
2. **He did not say** that the two ways are equal in what they teach. He said they give different nuances.
3. **The model he corrects:** mine. I treated the agent instance as needing a new word because "game" seemed false for many games. A tournament is a game; the player is the same agent throughout. The word needed is for the TIER, not for a new kind of being.
4. **What replaces it:**
   - one ARC game is a game;
   - a run of linked games played in one go is a game one tier up, a tournament;
   - a single-game instance is a tournament of one;
   - the agent (the player) is the same throughout: one lifetime.
   - The two modes are two schedules for the same player: one game per instance with a save between (spaced: a pause to absorb), or many games in one instance (massed: the story guides).
5. **Where it lands:** the instance word in the "life" sweep; and a design question about what each schedule teaches.

## TETHER (primary, verbatim)

- **Same player, different tiers.** Fig 4 :19-20: "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."
  -> One game or a tournament: the same loop at a larger size. The player does not change.
- **What crosses a pause.** Fig 12 :107-110: "where the arrangement is recorded, re-deriving it is a lookup and the actions were spent once ... Inside a run the arrangement persists and nothing is re-earned; across a boundary that drops it, everything is."
  -> Straight through, everything held stays live (the story guides).
- **What a pause keeps.** Fig 4 :22-24: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down ... Never the recording of a particular success." Fig 10 :68: "a generator crosses, an instance does not".
  -> Across a pause, only what was compressed into a method crosses. The pause forces the absorbing and contextualizing: what survives it is what generalizes. That is the spaced reader's gain.
- **The two schedules teach different things, and both are the same loop.** Fig 4 :27: "The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."
  -> The pause loses detail and keeps method. The straight-through keeps detail and risks carrying "the recording of a particular success" (Fig 4 :23).

## WORLD (secondary, each source read)

- **Massed vs spaced viewing.** Horvath, Horton, Lodge & Hattie 2017 (*First Monday* 22(9)):
  - "binge watching leads to strong memory formation immediately following program viewing";
  - "these memories decay more rapidly than memories formed after daily- or weekly-episode viewing schedules";
  - binge watchers "reported significantly less show enjoyment".
  -> Predicts Isaiah's point exactly: the same viewer learns differently by schedule. Straight-through is stronger now and fades faster; paced is slower and lasts. Corroborates.
- **Spacing.** Cepeda, Pashler, Vul, Wixted & Rohrer 2006 (*Psychological Bulletin* 132(3):354-380), comparing "consecutive massed presentations vs. spaced learning episodes": "ISI and retention interval operate jointly to affect final-test retention"; "the ISI producing maximal retention increased as retention interval increased."
  -> The longer the knowledge must last, the more the gaps help. Corroborates the spaced reader's gain.

No conflict found.

3. **Design question (nothing built):** the two schedules are not equivalent learners.
   - One game per instance forces a pause at every game: only what was saved crosses, which is consolidation.
   - Straight-through keeps everything live across games: momentum, but also the risk of carrying one game's particular success into the next (Fig 4 :23).
   - Worth a read later: in a tournament, does the agent get its "pause" at each game's end (the ending saves write, but does anything compress or contextualize between games)? And do the two schedules leave different libraries?
