# WATSON 2026-11-09 -- Transcribe before compressing; a guess is not a rule; inference fills the gap

Isaiah, 12:10 CST, on "the agent holds both at once ... nothing reconciles them":

"This is Ship of Theseus type stuff ... Both are true and neither are true ... Basically, time is the state that is muddying the waters here. In a non-linear sense, both are true facts as 'snapshots' of that point in time. Had more 'complete' information been collected, we would also see the 'conditions that caused the change for each'. Just because the agent or its instruments/perception missed what caused the conditions and did not capture them to save does not mean that they were not present. A part of learning involves transcription, not narration/summarization. That 'right/privilege' of compression only works when the agent fully understands the system and then can use those compressions properly. At any other point, it would be a misread. So two things are at fault here: the agent treats everything it guesses as rules or greater, and has skipped past inference, deduction, abduction, etc."

## Unpacked (the five-step rule)

1. **He said:**
   - (i) There is no real contradiction. Each observation is true AT ITS TIME. Time is the state that was dropped, and with it each moment's conditions.
   - (ii) Conditions the agent failed to perceive or save were still present.
   - (iii) Learning starts with TRANSCRIPTION (what happened, with its conditions), not summary. Compression is a privilege earned by understanding; done early, it is a misread.
   - (iv) Two faults: the agent promotes guesses to rules, and it skips the reasoning modes (inference, deduction, abduction) between observing and concluding.
2. **He did not say** that compression is bad. It is the goal, once earned.
3. **The model he corrects:** mine, again. I called it "a contradiction the agent can't reconcile". It is two snapshots whose time and conditions were stripped. The agent compressed "row stays" from ONE snapshot (c47) and stored the claim without the conditions that made it true then.
4. **What replaces it:**
   - a record of each observation with its time and conditions, kept as a transcript;
   - a claim's strength earned across varied snapshots;
   - a miss starts the inference cycle (abduce a condition, deduce a prediction, test it by acting) before any claim is compressed or strengthened.
5. **Where it lands:**
   - D2 (now: how a claim's strength moves);
   - what a term's evidence stores (a count, or the transcribed snapshots with their conditions);
   - the order of reasoning when a miss happens.

## WORLD (secondary, each source read)

- **Logic of inquiry: the missing modes, in order.** Peirce, via the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Abduction" (supplement on Peirce):
  - "The surprising fact, C, is observed. But if A were true, C would be a matter of course. Hence, there is reason to suspect that A is true." (CP 5.189)
  - "[a]bduction is the process of forming explanatory hypotheses" (CP 5.172); deduction helps "derive testable consequences"; induction "finally helps us to reach a verdict on the hypotheses."
  -> Here: C = o0's row changed on 9 cycles. Abduce A = "a vertical move into an open cell changes the row". Deduce: "move up now and the row drops by one". Induce: act and see. The agent jumped from one observation to a settled claim with none of the three. Corroborates fault (b).
- **Neuroscience: fast verbatim episodes first, slow generalization after.** McClelland, McNaughton & O'Reilly 1995 (*Psychological Review* 102:419-457):
  - "The hippocampal system permits rapid learning of new items without disrupting this structure";
  - "the neocortex learns slowly to discover the structure in ensembles of experiences", which works "if learning of each item is gradual and interleaved";
  - "Attempts to learn new information rapidly in a network that has previously learned a subset of some domain lead to catastrophic interference."
  -> Predicts exactly Isaiah's (iii): transcribe episodes as they are; compress only across many interleaved episodes; compressing early corrupts what is known. Corroborates.

No conflict found.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

1. **No paradox.** Both snapshots are true at their times. The "contradiction" is an artefact of a record that dropped time and conditions (Symbols :88; Fig 6 :47, "two scopes"). I withdraw "nothing reconciles them". What reconciles them is the conditions, which were never transcribed.
2. **Unperceived conditions are residual** (Fig 11 :48), owed to perception. They are never a reason to conclude "nothing caused it".
3. **Transcription first, compression earned:**
   - a claim's evidence should be the snapshots with their conditions (Fig 13 :130, :140-141), not a hit/miss tally;
   - a claim compressed from one occasion fails the bargain as the figure states it (Fig 5 :44), so it should never have settled.
4. **The two faults, as the figures name them:**
   - (a) STRENGTH INFLATION: settling on one occasion puts a guess at "settled". On the strength spectrum it is a guess until varied snapshots raise it.
   - (b) SKIPPED INFERENCE: a miss should run verdict -> lookup -> elimination (Fig 9 :43), i.e. abduce a condition, deduce its prediction, test it by acting, before the claim moves at all.
5. **For the build (questions only, nothing built; Isaiah's confirmation first):**
   - (i) what does a term's evidence store today: per-cycle snapshots with conditions (action, position, blocked cells), or counts?
   - (ii) does anything let a term settle on a single occasion, and where?
   - (iii) when a prediction misses, is there any step that proposes a condition (abduction) before standing changes?
   The seat's c34 condition read (r1567 3) already tests (iii) on one case. (i) and (ii) are the next read.

- **Cost if wrong:** more memory per term, from transcribed snapshots, and slower settling. Both are bounded, and the speed cost is the price of not misreading.
- **Undoable:** yes; it is design and reads first.
- **Reversed by:** the read showing terms already keep conditioned snapshots and settle only across varied occasions. Then the fault is elsewhere, in the condition search alone.
