## TETHER (primary, verbatim)

1. **A frequency is a recording; a mechanism is a method.**
   - Fig 4 :22-26: "Only methods cross up ... upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success ... A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."
   - Fig 10 :68: "a generator crosses, an instance does not".
   - A tally of how often something happened is a summary of the occasions seen. It describes those occasions. A mechanism, the "why", is a generator: it can be reapplied to a situation that was never seen.
2. **The why is the arrangement, and the arrangement is what carries.**
   - Fig 13 :130: "A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."
   - Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."
   - Knowing why means holding the bonds: what acts on what, in which order. A frequency holds only that two things co-occurred, with no bond.
3. **Frequency cannot see the condition that makes it hold.**
   - Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome."
   - Fig 9 :55-56: an outcome is often a compound of conditions; "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing".
   - A pattern counted across mixed situations averages over whether the fixing condition was present. Change the mix, and the frequency moves while the mechanism does not.
4. **A frequency is a filter, never a verdict.** Fig 9 :60, as in the previous WATSON.

Triangulated: Fig 4 / Fig 10 (method vs recording), Fig 13 / Fig 12 (the arrangement is the term), Fig 12 :123 / Fig 9 (the fixing condition). Low overlap.

## WORLD (secondary, each source read)

- **Pearl's ladder of causation**, via Wikipedia "Causal model":
  - "The lowest level, Association (seeing/observing), entails the sensing of regularities or patterns in the input data";
  - "The middle level, Intervention (doing), predicts the effects of deliberate actions";
  - "The highest level, Counterfactuals (imagining), involves constructing a theory of (part of) the world" that explains "why specific actions have specific effects and what happens in the absence of such actions."
  - Predicts: frequency is the bottom rung. It cannot answer "what if I act" or "what if things were different". Isaiah's ranking is the ladder's ranking. Corroborates.
- **The Lucas critique**, Lucas 1976, via Wikipedia: "any change in policy will systematically alter the structure of econometric models." The relationships estimated from past data stop holding when the conditions change, and the remedy is to model the underlying decision rules.
  - Predicts: a frequency learned under one set of conditions breaks when the agent itself changes the conditions, which is exactly what an agent acting in a game does. The why survives the change; the pattern does not. Corroborates, and gives the reason.
- **The base rate fallacy**, via Wikipedia: "people tend to ignore the base rate (e.g., general prevalence) in favor of the information pertaining only to a specific case."
  - Predicts the CORRECTION below: there are situations where frequency is the right thing to use and ignoring it is the error.

## VERDICT, with the nuance Isaiah asked for

**Isaiah's point holds, and here is the reason in words:** a frequency only says "this went with that, under the conditions I happened to see". A why says "this produces that, because of how they are joined". Three things follow:
1. **The why transfers; the frequency does not.** The why is a method (Fig 4), so it applies to a board never seen. The frequency describes the boards seen.
2. **The why survives the agent's own actions.** An agent changes the conditions every time it acts. A frequency collected before is then measured on a different world (Lucas). The mechanism is what still holds.
3. **The why says what to change.** It names the fixing condition (Fig 12 :123), so the agent knows which lever to pull. A frequency cannot separate the lever from what merely came along with it: the 50 switch flips.

**The corrections (where frequency still earns its place):**
- (a) **When no mechanism is known yet, frequency is the right first ranking.** It beats both random choice and guessing at a "why" the agent cannot yet test. Ignoring it is the base-rate fallacy. So frequency is not worthless. It is the starting rung: it orders what to test, the filter role (Fig 9 :60).
- (b) **An untested "why" is worse than an honest frequency.** A story that explains everything without being tested is the "explains everything" failure (Fig 5 :43). The why earns its rank only when it is tested by intervention (doing, Mill's difference) and holds.
- (c) **In a world that really is random** (a dice roll, a shuffled deck), there is no mechanism below the frequency to find. Frequency is then the whole truth. That is rare in designed puzzles like ARC, which is why Isaiah's "in most cases" is right for this agent.

**For the agent, concretely:**
- B2's validity tally (frequency) ranks which kind to try. It never stands as the reason.
- A sense cluster's standing should come from tested mechanism (an intervention that produced the predicted outcome, and its counterfactual) over raw hit counts. That is the ladder: seeing ranks, doing confirms, imagining explains.
- This goes into the D1/D2 and "B2 one history" designs as a constraint: a mechanism tested by difference outranks any frequency. Frequency decides only between untested options.

- **Cost if wrong:** over-trusting a mechanism tested once. Mitigated by (b): a why stands only while its tests hold.
- **Undoable:** yes; this is a design constraint.
- **Reversed by:** the retest showing frequency-ranked choices winning more often than mechanism-ranked ones on unseen boards.
