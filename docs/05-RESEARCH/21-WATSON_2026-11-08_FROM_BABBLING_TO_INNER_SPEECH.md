## QUESTION

A human mind moves from pre-verbal, contact-based knowing, to composing words, to thinking in language. Where are the transition points? And what does each one say about how the agent should GENERATE goal guesses from cues and contact?

Today the agent fills every grammar template for every pair of objects, then picks one. Isaiah has called that a multiple-choice test, not an answer written in full (11-07).

## DOMAIN CENSUS

- **R:**
  - Arity: cues and vocabulary in, one guess out.
  - Symmetry: two guesses that differ only in which object fills a slot are ONE thought, with the instance bound last.
  - Scale: the step (generation), the game (standing), and the lineage (what crosses).
- **The perceptual channel:**
  - actors: objects, changes, contact;
  - currency: residual;
  - anchor: the frames.
- **The vocabulary (the library):**
  - actors: atoms, templates ("verbs"), recipes;
  - currency: standing earned by endings;
  - anchor: the ledger.
- **The generator (thinking):**
  - actors: goal guesses;
  - currency: bits to state them and actions to test them (Fig 12);
  - anchor: the board's verdict.
- **The lineage:**
  - actors: methods and recordings;
  - anchor: Fig 4.
- **Union:** vocabulary ∩ generator is a compound: which words the agent may reach for in this situation. This is the link the question is about.
- **Order (Fig 3):** perception, then vocabulary, then objective, then planning, then learn-and-carry.

## TETHER (primary, verbatim)

1. **The link that breaks is vocabulary-to-objective.**
   - Fig 3 :6-7: "2 · vocabulary / it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked".
   - Fig 3 :9-10: "3 · the objective / it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms".
   - Fig 3 :25-27: "The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither [failures nor tests], so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments. A link with no instrument is not a link that works."
   - So vocabulary is AFFORDANCE terms, and the transition the question asks about is exactly Fig 3's link 2 into link 3.
2. **An instrument grows from a worse instrument that already returns something.**
   - Fig 6 :32-33: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something. Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars."
   - So language is not installed whole. It is bootstrapped from a working pre-verbal channel (contact), each stage sharpening the last.
3. **The lookup returns a kind, keyed by the described effect.**
   - Fig 9 :25-32: "one lookup / R's own predicates name the habitat that holds φ* / describe it before you go looking ... matching is a one-pass check, not a search / your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause / → you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself".
   - So the cue (the effect in contact now) selects the KIND of thought. The instance is bound after.
4. **A term earns its place only by the bargain, and only from what is held.**
   - Fig 5 :22-25: "can we even build this term? / it composes from what we already have / is it actually new? / it is not already in the library".
   - Fig 5 :43-44: "A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."
   - So a generalisation, a schema over many items, is admitted only when stating it costs less than its instances. A single success is not a rule.
5. **A guess should be written as an ordinary term that can be wrong.**
   - Fig 5 :65-66: "make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else ... Committing a prediction before a run is this, already working."
   - So a goal guess is a stated prediction, a sentence, that the board can refute.
6. **Methods cross; recordings do not.**
   - Fig 4 :22-26: "Only methods cross up ... Never the recording of a particular success ... Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."
   - So imitation, replaying a chunk, is the starting instrument and never the end state. What should cross is the schema.
7. **Variation inside, selection between.**
   - Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
   - So many guesses may be offered. Which kind of guess gets offered again is decided by endings.

Triangulated: Fig 3 and Fig 9 (the link and the lookup), Fig 5 and Fig 4 (admission and crossing), Fig 6 (bootstrapping). These have low overlap.

## WORLD (secondary, each source read)

- **T0, meaning before words.** Mandler, "How to build a baby II: Conceptual primitives", *Psychological Review* 99, 1992.
  - "A mechanism of perceptual analysis by which infants derive meaning from perceptual activity."
  - "Image-schemas create conceptual structure from the spatial structure of objects and their movements."
  - Image-schemas form "an interface between the continuous processes of perception and the discrete nature of language". Her named examples are animacy, agency and containment.
  - Predicts: the agent's pre-verbal layer is its attributes, changes and contact levels. These are the umwelt's head start (Fig 4). Corroborates.
- **T1, finding units in the stream.** Saffran, Aslin & Newport, 1996, via Wikipedia "Statistical learning in language acquisition": 8-month-olds, after "two minutes" of nonsense speech, learned "which syllables are always paired together and which ones only occur together relatively rarely".
  - Predicts: the first units come from what reliably changes TOGETHER. In the agent that is co-change contact (L2), and Fig 5's "gap that is large and compressible". Corroborates.
- **T2, binding a word to a thing through shared attention.** Carpenter, Nagell & Tomasello, *Monographs SRCD*, 1998: "Infants progressed from sharing to following to directing others' attention". Time "in joint engagement" with the mother predicted earliest communication.
  - Predicts: words bind through PROXIMITY and attention, not by trying every referent. In the agent, a term binds to what is in contact now. Corroborates (Isaiah's proximity point).
  - Also, from the same Wikipedia article: word meanings may be learned by "propose-but-verify" (Trueswell et al. 2013): hold one guess, test it, keep or drop it. That corroborates Fig 5 :65-66.
- **T3 and T4, imitation, then item-based islands.** Tomasello, "The item-based nature of children's early syntactic development", *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 4(4), 2000:
  - "initially, imitative learning is all that children do";
  - "each of their verbs forms its own island of organization";
  - "there appeared to be no transfer of structure across verbs";
  - with a novel verb, young children "much more often matched the ordering patterns they had heard";
  - children "categorize, schematize and creatively combine these individually learned expressions".
  - Predicts: generation is LOCAL to each verb, filled only in the slots that verb has been seen working with. It never runs every verb across every argument pair. Corroborates, and it is the sharpest prediction for the agent.
- **T5, abstraction after a critical mass.** Same paper: abstract schemas "emerge only gradually and in piecemeal fashion", and "there must be some 'critical mass' of exemplars".
  - McMurray, *Science*, 2007: the vocabulary explosion "is a by-product of parallel learning and variation in the time to learn words ... Specialized processes are not causally necessary to explain acceleration."
  - Marcus et al., 1992 (PubMed abstract): overregularization is rare ("median 2.5%"). Children hold memorised irregulars plus a rule, and "Retrieval of an irregular blocks the rule."
  - Predicts:
    - a template generalises to new kinds only after several item successes;
    - the speed-up comes free from many templates learning in parallel, so build no special "spurt" mechanism;
    - a specific remembered case blocks the general rule, and rare over-generalisations are the visible sign a rule exists.
  - Corroborates Fig 5's bargain and Fig 4's crossing of methods.
- **T6, private speech becomes inner speech.** Vygotsky, *Thinking and Speech*, 1934/1987, via PsychologyNotesHQ:
  - social speech "emerging around a child's second year";
  - private speech "between approximately ages 3 and 7", "audible self-talk" used to direct behaviour, and "children talk to themselves most during genuinely difficult tasks" (Berk 1986);
  - inner speech "around ages 7 to 8", "radically compressed", "predicates without subjects".
  - Predicts:
    - the agent's NSM narration is its private speech, and should regulate action MOST when stuck;
    - as competence grows, internal planning compresses to predicates over the subject already held in context.
  - Corroborates Fig 5 :65-66 (a stated plan accrues residual) and Fig 4 :27 (the transform is lossy by design).

No conflict found. One nuance is reported, not averaged: cross-situational STATISTICAL word learning is contested (Trueswell 2013 favours propose-but-verify). The figures side with propose-but-verify (Fig 5 :65-66).

## THE TRANSITION POINTS, mapped to the agent

| Stage | Human | Agent today | What it implies |
|---|---|---|---|
| T0 meaning before words | image schemas from objects and movement | attributes, deltas, kinds, contact levels L0-L5 | in place; the umwelt's head start |
| T1 units from co-occurrence | syllables that always go together | co-change contact (L2) is read | units of thought come from what changes together |
| T2 binding by attention | joint attention, proximity | contact picks the subject class (S0/S1/S2) and the contact level | a template's slots are filled from what is in contact, closest first |
| T3 imitation | whole chunks reproduced | preloaded recipes and replays | a starting instrument, never the end (Fig 4) |
| **T4 item-based islands** | each verb its own island, used only in heard orders | **MISSING: every template is filled for every pair** | **generate only from templates with a history for THIS contact kind and subject class; bind the slots from contact** |
| T5 abstraction by critical mass | schemas after many exemplars; specifics block rules | B2 learns per contact kind (3-hold threshold); steer item 27 keeps standing per (template form, slot kind), but only to penalise | a template is offered beyond its learned kinds only after several paid endings, or as logged exploration when nothing local applies |
| T6 private, then inner speech | self-talk on hard tasks, then compressed predicates | NSM trace narrates; musing ruled 11-05 | narrate to steer when stuck; compress as competence grows |

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The agent's thinking sits BEFORE T4. Filling every template across every pair is not even imitation. It is babbling across the whole combinatorial space, then choosing.

The figures and the developmental record agree on the next transition: generation becomes ITEM-BASED.
- The cue in contact selects the KIND of thought (Fig 9).
- Only templates with standing for that kind are reached for (Fig 3's affordance terms; Fig 5's "composes from what we already have").
- Their slots bind to objects in contact, closest first.
- A template earns reach to new kinds only by the bargain, after a critical mass of paid endings (Fig 5 :43-44; Fig 4 :22).
- The wide fill stays, but only as System 0's last resort when no item-based template applies, logged as exploration ("random is the LAST resort", Isaiah 09-21).

**This is the roadmap's "widened steer", read correctly.** Steer item 27 already records standing per (template form, slot kind). Today it only PENALISES (r1415). Widening it to ORDER and LIMIT generation by that standing is T4 and T5.

Three design items for Isaiah's approval before the seat builds them (11-05: he wants to be asked before agency changes):
- **D1, verb islands.** Generation proposes from templates with standing for the contact kinds and subject classes present. Slots bind from contact order. Standing is learned from endings, own history mixed with inherited by w.
- **D2, critical mass and blocking.** A template reaches a new kind only after k paid endings on its learned kinds, or as logged exploration when nothing local applies. A specific remembered case beats the general template where both apply.
- **D3, private speech when stuck.** The NSM trace steers at difficulty, through musing. Internal plans compress to predicates over the held subject as competence rises.

- **Cost if wrong:** moderate. Generation could miss a paying goal outside the learned islands.
- **Undoable:** yes, under arms, with the wide fill kept as the fallback.
- **Reversed by:** the online retest. Paying goals found by the wide fill that item-based generation never offers, even through its exploration fallback, would mean the islands are too narrow, or that k is too high.
