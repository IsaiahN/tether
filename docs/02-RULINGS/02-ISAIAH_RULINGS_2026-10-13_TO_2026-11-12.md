# ISAIAH'S RULINGS, 2026-10-13 TO 2026-11-12

This continues `01-ISAIAH_RULINGS` (to 2026-10-12). These are the designer's rulings on how the agent should work, in the order they were made. Where a later ruling replaces an earlier one, the later one is marked "supersedes". The Tether figures outrank everything here (authority order: figures and the Operators/Symbols tables, then these rulings, then the corpus, then the seats).

## The agent's nature

- The agent is a general agent that exists beyond any one challenge. ARC's actions are challenge-specific quirks; a general agent is plug and play, with a learning curve, as humans are (11-09).
- It thinks in intentions, not buttons. The interface (its "limbs") translates intent and direction into whatever inputs exist, and must tell the agent useful things upward, such as that something isn't possible, the way skin reports temperature without the brain knowing the mechanics (10-23, 10-25).
- What the agent cares about is progress toward its intention. If blocked, it finds another route; which actuator gets credit is irrelevant to reasoning (10-23).
- Agency comes first. Options and priors are wired in as defaults, but the agent learns what is right in each situation from many environments. It is never handed a decision tree (11-05).
- No scaffolding or outside coaching: it is the first of its kind and must teach itself or learn from other umwelts (10-30).
- One lifetime. Every save is the same agent's memory; the library is its mind and never resets (10-14, 10-19, 11-08). A game's run is an "attempt", what ends it is a "game loss", never "death" or a "life" lost (11-09). Linked games played in one go are a "game series" (11-09).
- The agent must never be told a game's real name; learning has to transfer by concept, not by game (10-25). No game-specific hard-coding anywhere, even as defaults (11-01, 11-02).
- Its priors are innate: all atoms are settled by default; only new atoms and recipes are earned in play (10-14). The priors are one era's human knowledge, dated but not set in stone (11-10).
- It needs no human to keep evolving: it creates, reasons and self-determines, changing through contact with environments (11-09). Diversity is life; homogeneity leads to stagnation in any substrate (11-09).
- On endings: give the agent the truth about what ending means for its own umwelt; neither avoid it nor run toward it; a reasoning agent need not fear death but obsolescence (11-09).

## Reasoning, claims and contradiction

- Claims have a strength, a gradient rather than a ladder: hunch, guess, hypothesis, theory, rule, law. The only laws the agent need concern itself with are the Tether's (11-09).
- A held claim and observations against it are each true snapshots at their time; the dropped state is time and its conditions. So a contradiction means conditions not yet known: adjust the guesses, and find the conditions by testing, inference and observation (11-09). Contradicted claims are weakened along the strength range and crowded out, never deleted, until the arrangement changes (11-09).
- Learning is transcription, not summary. The right to compress is earned only once the system is understood (11-09).
- Acting on a contradiction looks like a person's response: examine the idea, backtrack, recall the scenario, check whether the world changed or the order was wrong (10-17).
- Inferred (hidden) causes and look-ahead are part of the human umwelt and belong in the agent (11-02).
- Causation needs more than counting: correlation is required but not enough, and must be tested from several angles. Knowing why beats frequency (11-09).
- Responses range from reflex to reaction to action to strategy (multi-step, layered, with contingencies) (11-09).
- Reasoning doesn't require inner speech; the outcome is what is saved and compressed (11-08). The agent should still "speak in both": it translates its reasoning into NSM so its thinking can be read like a chain of thought (11-03).
- The agent can think in the first person: "I want to GO to the restroom" uses AGENT|translate plus context, subtext and pretext (11-12).
- Self vs reflection: the cause-and-effect chain runs one way, y = f(x), and the agent is x. "You can't look externally for something outside you that is you", except when you see your own reflection (11-12).

## Search and thinking

- A lookup works from contact and description, never by reading everything (10-31). Search is a tag/attribute-ranked lookup, like categories in a blog (11-02); like a search engine, dynamically reweighted, with results cached until the library changes (11-06).
- Tiered search stops at a tier and keeps using it until something changes; never one entry at a time (11-02).
- Minting by blind enumeration is a combinatorial explosion (11-01). Thinking that uses the library is a search problem and must be optimized so that even 5-10 thoughts are the most likely ones, ranked by the "shape of the answer" (11-12). Fixed budgets are magic numbers (10-21, 11-06, 11-12).
- Categorize, relate, anticipate: filing by described effect, nearness from use, anticipating the next search, PageRank-style ordering, approved as one package (11-08).
- Each operator and NSM kind gets a THESAURUS; each group in it is a SENSE CLUSTER (names chosen 11-08, superseding "catalog" and "word cluster").
- A kind is set by its minimum defining attributes: "a door with a different handle is still a door" (11-11).
- Contact is graded, not yes/no (touching, or affecting indirectly), and ordered, with the order adjusted by history (11-07, 11-08).
- Goal choice is ordered by efficacy, never by text order; random draws are a last resort, only for true unknowns (11-08).
- The agent leads a moving target: it acts on where a mover will be, not where it was seen (11-08).

## Effort, time and the think-or-act choice

- The agent justifies the time each step takes by how much the situation changed; without a reason there is no reason to spend it (10-31). Musing is a reasoning act the agent chooses for itself (11-05).
- Thinking is never free, even untimed; too much is analysis paralysis (11-06). One currency for thinking, at the agent's own current pace (11-07).
- A step should take at most 20-30 seconds (10-31); System 0 under about 5 seconds, as a gauge, not a magic number (10-31).
- Leaving a line of work works like map rerouting: estimate the slowdown, reroute past a threshold, reroute at once for a closed road, weighed by deadline and stakes (11-06).
- Needs before curiosity, like Maslow's hierarchy, as a dial (11-06).
- No orchestrator or governor gets the credit; the room does the work (11-07). "Governor" was renamed.

## Memory, fading and the library

- Store the concept, not every apple (11-02). Repeated shapes become kinds the agent names itself, unconfused by colour, position or rotation (11-02).
- Each memory layer is reweighted as events come in; things never called fall out of search, useful ones move up (11-07). Forgetting acts on reach (tier, retrieval cost, compression), never on earned counts (11-07).
- Recall of "I just saw this" is a temporary cache; the library is long-term memory with its own compression (11-10).
- Successes can fade as well as failures. The switch is named for the difference: GENERAL agent (fades) vs SPECIALIST agent (doesn't) (11-10).
- Fading never runs on clock time. It advances with what gets written into memory, as a share: an item's age is the share of everything stored after its last use (11-10). A still board where nothing new is written fades nothing (11-11).
- A habit failing again after old failures faded is a reassessment by the present self, not a reinstatement (11-10). Failure records are kind-based, never name-based (11-10).
- Second nature: praise fades and moves up to bigger expectations; routine holds don't count as reinforcement (11-11). The reuse count decides what is loaded at game start and kept ready, counted per task (the sense cluster), and only during a window of chances that is the same for every item (11-11).
- Storage: one central library, deduplicated and tallied; SQLite by default, JSON also built; data from test runs is merged into central, never kept in fragments (10-24, 11-02, 11-03). For experiments, make a new library and never delete the old one: "tests as branches, but for data" (11-10).
- On merge uncertainty, take the certain tally (11-03).

## Features and switches

- Features stay on. Turning one off is like cutting off a limb: only with extreme scrutiny (10-26). Test comparisons decide how a feature is refined, never whether it ships (11-03).
- Recovery fixes (partial progress, level-change correction) are on by default (11-09).
- Learned weights are plain numbers; when one changes mid-game, the change should be visible (11-06).

## Testing

- Build first; avoid "death by test" (11-09). No long runs while major functionality is shifting (11-11). Playing games counts as testing (11-10).
- After the step 8 ship: the test-learn-diagnose loop, cycles kept only while productive, with a noise-floor baseline and four experiments (library wipe, cold start vs lineage, general vs specialist, held-out transfer) (11-10).
- Changes in the cycles must be general (wiring), never toward particular games; anything near a "page or chapter" change goes to the designer first (11-10).
