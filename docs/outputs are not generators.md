The prevailing bet in artificial intelligence is that a sufficiently large volume of human text, compressed and scaled, arrives at general and eventually superhuman reasoning.

A corpus is the compressed output of human minds; the capacity that produced it is a generator, and outputs do not contain their generator. Decompressing every recorded human thought therefore reaches the ceiling of current human reasoning and no further, because the engine that improves human reasoning, discovery under real consequence, was never in the text.

The argument descends through several layers, ending at a problem the field has not yet named: compression is shaped by the sensory priors of the compressor, all the way down to its dreams, so there is no neutral representation to aim at, and every reasoner, biological, artificial, or composed, can only be surprised along axes its primitives can already represent. The escape is not more data. It is a built discoverer, and a way to import not new terms but new axes.

1. The Gambit
The claim under examination is not a strawman and should not be attacked as one. Nobody serious argues that a corpus of human text outpaces evolution, or that aggregation exceeds its own source. Those are trivially false, a shadow cannot be taller than the thing that casts it, and defeating them proves nothing.

The real bet is narrower and not foolish. Evolution spent roughly four billion years building minds that can reason. Those minds spent the last several thousand years writing down the results. A machine can absorb that written output in a single training run rather than re-running either process. The corpus, on this view, is not a rival to evolution; it is evolution’s finished homework, delivered without the wait. A human takes twenty years to become competent by slowly decompressing a fraction of the corpus through reading; a machine decompresses all of it at once.

Stated this way, the bet is respectable. The disagreement is not about whether inheriting compressed output is powerful, it is. The disagreement is about what the output contains, and specifically whether it contains the thing that would be needed to exceed its source.

2. Outputs are not generators
A written sentence is a lossy compression of the reasoning that produced it. The author’s mind did the work, association, inference, structural assembly, and the text is the residue that survived being written down. When a model trains on text, it is not learning to think; it is learning the compressed traces of many minds’ outputs and reconstructing the distribution that generated them.

This reframes the much-discussed data wall. The wall is not running out of words. It is running out of compressed human cognition to decompress. And it explains model collapse, the degradation of models trained on prior models’ output, from first principles: model-generated text is a compression of a model’s reconstruction, so training on it is decompressing a decompression, a copy of a copy. Each generation carries less original cognition and more prior-model reconstruction. The decay is generational recompression loss.

The corpus contains the problems evolution already solved and someone wrote down. It does not contain the capacity to solve new ones, because that capacity lives in the living process, the mind generating, not in the text it generated. The text is the frozen output of an ongoing process, and inheriting the output is not inheriting the process.

This is the load-bearing move of the entire argument, so it is worth stating without ornament. To exceed human reasoning you need a better engine. A pile of human reasoning’s outputs is not an engine. You cannot accumulate your way from the category of outputs to the category of generators; they are different kinds of thing, and no quantity of the first sums to the second.

3. Aggregation has a ceiling, and it is human
Put a hundred geniuses in a room. The output is not super-genius. Aggregation buys breadth (more fields, more perspectives) and parallelism (more problems at once); it does not buy depth on a single hard novel problem. Often it is worse there, because a committee regresses toward consensus, and consensus is the average, which is duller than the best member on the thing the best member is best at.

A model trained on the aggregate of human output is that room made permanent: breadth no human possesses, and depth capped at roughly the aggregate’s competence on any given novel thing. Diminishing returns on more data is real and it is the same principle, the hundred-and-first genius adds almost nothing the first hundred did not cover. The data wall is the point at which the next genius is redundant.

The one existence proof, and how to read it
The scaling camp has one genuine counter: a single human brain is also aggregated units, no neuron is smart, you cannot get intelligence by averaging neurons, and yet enough of them, wired richly enough, produce something no neuron contains. Intelligence emerged from aggregation-at-scale in the one case we know. So “aggregation cannot exceed its parts” is false for brains.

But the lesson is misread. Brains did not become intelligent by aggregating more neurons, a whale has more neurons than a human and is not smarter. Brains became intelligent by structural reorganization under selection: the architecture changed, not merely the count. So the honest lesson from the one existence proof is not scale causes emergence, it is the right structure causes emergence, and scale only helps once the structure is right.

Adding data and parameters to a fixed architecture is the whale strategy: more units, same structure, diminishing returns. The escape is not more of the same substrate. It is a change of structure, which is a bet on architecture, not on scale.

4. The generator is discovery under consequence
If the corpus is outputs and the engine is a generator, what is the generator, mechanically? Human reasoning was produced by selection over populations across deep time, an engine that generates new reasoning capacity. Humans do not get smarter from the text; they got smart from selection pressure over billions of individuals across millennia. The text is the current generation’s output; the improvement engine is selection, and selection is not in the text and never was.

This tempts a strong conclusion, that only biological embodiment and evolution can reach superhuman reasoning. The conclusion overreaches, but pursuing why it overreaches sharpens what actually is required.

Novelty is a kind of failure, not a kind of success
It is objected that humans cannot reliably reason on novel structure either, and this is true, and it exposes a real flaw in how the target is usually set. Most human “reasoning on the novel” is analogy to the familiar; genuine novel-structure reasoning, with no analogy available, is rare and hard for us. So a target of reliable novel-structure reasoning is one humans do not clear, and holding a machine to it is incoherent.

But the symmetry breaks on the shape of failure. A human facing novel structure builds a partial model, tests it, notices it is wrong, and revises, even in failure, the mind moves; the failure is a search. A corpus model facing novel structure produces a fluent, confident, wrong answer and gains no traction over attempts, because there is no search, one forward pass pattern-matches to the nearest familiar thing and confabulates. The human fails by searching; the decompressor fails by guessing.

General intelligence is not reliable success on the novel, nobody has that. It is the right kind of failure: making progress through search-and-adaptation rather than confabulation. The bar is whether failure accumulates traction, not whether the answer arrives.

What discovery requires: a walk, and a model to be surprised against
Discovery of structure beyond what was already conceived reduces to two parts, and both are necessary. The first is a random walk, unguided contact with reality that samples configurations no hypothesis pointed at. “Discovery by accident” is not a separate, mystical route; stumbling is sampling, and enough unguided steps hit what no theory predicted. The great accidental discoveries, radioactivity from a fogged plate, the cosmic background mistaken for antenna noise, are the walk producing an unpredicted sample.

But the walk alone discovers nothing. Radioactivity was not discovered when the plate fogged; it was discovered when someone recognized the fogging as anomalous and did not stop. The second part is the recognizer: a model that the sample can violate, and the disposition to invest in the violation rather than discard it as noise. A walk with no model has no anomalies, only noise, you can only be surprised relative to an expectation.

Discovery under consequence = a random walk plus a model that can be surprised. Evolution is one implementation: variation is the walk, death is the recognizer, and a variant that survives an axis it never conceived is the recognition, done by consequence. Science is another: experiment is the walk, theory is the model, and the anomaly-chaser is the recognizer.

This dissolves the “only biology” overreach. The requirement is not embodiment-and-evolution specifically; it is discovery-under-consequence, and that has more than one substrate, biological selection, human science, and, in principle, a built agent acting under real stakes whose predictions can be violated by outcomes it did not foresee. Evolution proves a generator exists. It does not prove it is the only one.

5. The problem beneath the problem: compression is not neutral
Here the argument reaches the layer the field has not yet named, and it cuts at everyone, including the composed, corpus-free approach this thesis otherwise favors.

Compression is shaped by the sensory priors of the compressor. A snake with thermal vision does not merely see heat; it compresses the world along a thermal axis, so its concepts, its categories, its notion of similar-to-what, and its dreams, its offline recombination, run in thermal primitives. There is no neutral substrate underneath onto which the thermal is painted. The thermal is load-bearing all the way down. If dogs or other animals could write, their corpus would carry different primitives, different insights, different axes of similarity, because their compression scheme is downstream of a different sensorium.

Change the sensors, and not just the data changes, the shape of the compression changes: the categories, the primitives, what counts as similar to what. Even the dream is in thermal.

Why this is worse than the data wall
The data wall was: outputs do not contain the generator. This is deeper: every generator’s output is shaped by its sensory priors, so there is no view from nowhere to compress toward. The corpus models compress along human-language priors, which are themselves a compression along human-sensory priors, a compression of a compression, doubly filtered through the human window. No amount of human text contains a snake’s categories, and no scaling of visible-light primitives yields thermal ones.

And instruments do not fully escape it. A camera captures the bands it was built to capture; a spectrometer measures the range it was designed to measure. Every instrument is a crystallized hypothesis about what is worth measuring, and the hypothesis comes from human awareness. Instruments extend the sensory window but not the conceptual one, they reach what we cannot see but can imagine measuring. The truly hard case is reality’s structure along axes we neither sense nor know to look for, so we have built no instrument, so it has never been recorded, the unknown-unknowns of reality’s composition.

The recognizer only recognizes what its primitives can represent
This closes back onto the discoverer of Section 4, and tightens it. The recognizer can only register an anomaly it has the primitives to represent. A snake cannot be surprised by a color. A human cannot be surprised by a thermal gradient for which we have no primitive. A built agent cannot be surprised along an axis its type system cannot express. The invisibility is in the compression scheme, not the data, so reality’s structure along axes no primitive captures stays invisible to every discoverer at once, biological or built.

A random walk finds the unconceived only if the unconceived is expressible in the primitives the walker happens to hold. Reality’s structure along axes no primitive captures is invisible to everyone, and the invisibility cannot be fixed by more sampling, because it lives in the representation, not the sample.

6. What this does, and does not, settle
The corpus-dependent programs are bounded worst. Their compression is emergent, fixed, and human-language-shaped: they cannot extend their compression scheme without retraining from scratch, and the scheme they have is already twice-removed from reality. Piling up old books, buying, scanning, and consuming the last pre-synthetic human corpus, is the visible desperation of a program that senses the wall and mistakes it for a data shortage. It is not a data shortage. It is a category limit and, beneath that, a representation limit.

An approach that declares its primitives rather than growing them by training is bounded less, its compression scheme is legible and extensible; a primitive can be added deliberately where a trained network cannot add a sensory axis at all. But it is not bounded zero. Declared primitives are still a choice, and the choice is still drawn from human conception of what reality’s axes are. A minimum viable set of six terms is a human’s careful guess at reality’s compression axes; another sensorium’s minimum set need not be those six.

Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible, which is worth a great deal, because a visible choice can be revised and an emergent one cannot. But visibility is not neutrality, and revision is still bounded by what can be conceived to revise toward.

There is one honest way to carry human priors without contamination, and it is the discipline this thesis was written under: where a task is itself built on human priors, the priors are labeled and their provenance recorded, added because the task required them, marked as what they are, so a later reader can see exactly what was assumed and what was earned. Proof of provenance does not remove the priors. It makes them auditable, which is the most that honesty allows when the priors are unavoidable.

7. The frontier, named
The escape from the corpus is not more data; it is a built discoverer, a random walk under a model that can be surprised, run under real consequence, so that failure is a search and not a guess. That specification is substrate-neutral: it does not require re-running biology, only running the logic biology runs.

But the deepest limit, compression shaped by sensory priors, sets the discoverer a harder task than composing within a fixed set of primitives. The genuine reach past human conception requires an agent that discovers it needs a primitive it does not have: that hits a residual it cannot represent in its current scheme and responds by minting a new primitive, a new axis of compression, not merely a new composition within the old axes.

The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space. Importing a term extends what can be said; importing an axis extends what can be perceived at all, and only the second reaches structure the current primitives cannot represent.

This is unfired in every system now operating, and it is the same problem whether one is a laboratory scaling a corpus or an agent composing from the ground. Neither has shown an agent that grows its own representation in response to an anomaly it could not express. That is the frontier: not a bigger compression of the human window, but a mechanism for enlarging the window from inside, and the first requirement for building it is admitting the window is there.

Summary
The field believes it is worried about the right thing. It is buying books because it fears running out of text. But if the argument here holds, the shortage was never of text, it is of generators, and beneath that, of axes of representation. A program that understood the limit would not be grabbing the last pre-2022 written books. It would be studying animals, sensoria that compress reality along axes ours never had, because the scarce resource is not more of the human window, but any window that is not ours.
