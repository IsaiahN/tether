# The atom list — pruned and reorganised

Rewritten from the 539-element original. **Nothing is removed for being off-domain.** A human
who has never studied physics still knows a boat floats, and abstracts better for it.

## What was removed, and the one criterion

**An entry is out if no body could have found it.**

That is the line. It is not tier, not domain, not usefulness. It asks whether a person could
have arrived at the entry through contact, prediction and being wrong — the way a bathtub
teaches buoyancy. **Anything a body meets stays, whatever domain it was filed under.**

Which keeps `Entanglement` (a correlation that holds at a distance is a real thing to notice on
a board), keeps `Kolmogorov complexity` (the shortest description is a bias anyone has), and
keeps `Apophenia` — a term this project already uses.

And removes the entries that are labels for a feeling rather than operations: the eleven Tier-7
"ultimate syntheses", the deity vocabulary, and the retrocausal ones. **The full removal list is
at the end, with a reason each.**

## Tiers, and where they stop being derivations

Reading the original, the recipes change character partway up:

| tier | what a recipe is |
|---|---|
| **1** | a prior, cited to developmental or physical evidence |
| **2–3** | a derivation — the recipe either entails the element or it does not, and you can check |
| **4** | arguable — mostly entailment, some resemblance |
| **5** | resemblance more often than entailment |
| **6–7** | word association |

**That gradient is itself a finding: composition degrades at depth when nothing grounds it.**
The recipes stop being entailments and become resemblances, and the generator could not tell.

**So tiers 1–4 are kept in full, tier 5 is kept selectively, and tiers 6–7 are kept only where
the element is a real operation under a lurid name.**

## What this list is for

**The visible set, not the library.** Loading these as held terms would delete the evidence the
composition system works — the agent is supposed to reach for them, and reaching is the
evidence.

**A recipe is an aim point.** `Contain = Topology + Solidity` tells the agent what to compose,
not what to hold.

# TIER 1 — the foundational atoms

## Human — physics and cognition

| symbol | atom | what it says |
|---|---|---|
| **Co** | Cohesion | objects move as connected, bounded wholes |
| **Ct** | Continuity | objects travel connected, unobstructed paths |
| **So** | Solidity | two things cannot occupy one place |
| **Su** | Support | an unsupported thing falls; stability depends on contact |
| **An** | Animacy | motion without contact indicates an agent |
| **Fo** | Forward model | predict the sensory consequence of your own action, subtract it |

| **Ge** | Geometry | points, lines, angles, handedness |
| **To** | Topology | inside, outside, boundaries, holes |
| **Gs** | Gestalt | grouping by proximity, similarity, common fate |
| **St** | Statistics | sequence learning, transitional probabilities |
| **Ca** | Causality | covariation, intervention, launching |
| **Mv** | **Movement** | **a thing changes position** |

**`Movement` is a keystone and it is not derived.** It is one of the six semantic primes the
grammar still lacks — `DO`, `MOVE`, `HAPPEN`, `IF`, `BEFORE/AFTER`, `FOR SOME TIME` — and every
motion element below is `Movement` constrained by something else. **`Translate` is `Movement`
held to cohesion and continuity; `Flow` is `Movement` without cohesion; `Orbit` is `Movement`
about a fixed point.** Without it those are compositions with a missing operand.

| **Layer** | **Abut + Contr partial** | **one sits over the other; what is underneath persists, is partly readable, and changes what the top does** |

**`Amalgamate` assumed the fluid case and needed a companion.** *Two objects meeting become one*
is what happens when they are miscible — **and two rigid bodies meeting stack, abut or
interlock, and the boundary survives.** Whether merging is even available is **a fact about what
the things are made of**, and the agent has no way to say which world it is in without these.

**And `Layer` is the one that carries the most.** Wet into wet blends. Wet over dry occludes,
and the layer below is still there. **A glaze is neither — it transmits and tints, so both are
readable at once.** Same two colours, three outcomes, **decided by the state of what is
underneath.**

**Which is why they are three entries and not one.** `Merge` loses information irreversibly,
`Abut` loses none, **and `Layer` loses some and keeps the rest** — three different answers to
*what happened to the thing that was there before.*

**`Amalgamate` and `Separate` are the object-count pair**, and they are what `Bind` and
`Decompose` become when the subject is whole objects rather than features. **A board that merges
two things into one has done something the tracker must be able to say.**

**`Erase` and `Construct` are the other pair, and they violate permanence deliberately.** The
priors say objects do not cease to exist — **and a game can consume one, which is why
`consumed` is one of the seven affordance booleans.** A prior being violated is exactly the case
that needs its own name, because the alternative is a residual with nowhere to go.

**And `Phantom` is where perception and world come apart.** An after-image, a delayed effect, an
obstacle the model still holds after the board dropped it. **Distinct from `Camouflage`, which is
the world hiding something real** — this is the model holding something that is not there.

# DYNAMIC — what state a system is in

**The highest-value domain in this batch.** These are readings about a system rather than
operations on it, **and several of them name what the loop already does under other words.**

## Tier 1 — the dynamical atoms

| symbol | atom | what it says |
|---|---|---|
| **Stim** | Stimulus | input arriving; energy put into the system |
| **Resp** | Response | what the system does about it |
| **Var** | Variance **[dup]** | how much it fluctuates — also Raw data T1 |
| **Coup** | Coupling | how strongly parts interact; high syncs, low is independent |
| **Damp** | Damping | resistance to change |
| **Lat** | Latency | delay between a stimulus and its response |
| **Thr** | Threshold | the point at which behaviour changes abruptly |
| **Res** | Resource | consumable fuel that depletes with activity |
| **Sens** | Sensitivity | how much a small input amplifies |
| **Rec** | Recovery | how fast resource returns after a demand |

**`Variance` restates the raw-data atom.** One quantity, two uses.

**And `Latency` is worth its own line: the loop has no name for the delay between an action and
its effect**, which is why a lagged rule is one of the families outside closure.

## Tier 2 — the regimes

| element | recipe | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Rest** | Stim low + Var low + Damp high | stable, unchanging, unresponsive; nothing happens unless provoked |
| **Chaos** | Sens high + Coup high + Var extreme | deterministic and unpredictable; small differences explode; bounded, never repeats |
| Equilibrium | Stim = Resp + Var low | forces balance; no net change |
| Oscillation | Lat + Damp low + Coup feedback | periodic movement about a point |
| Quiescence | Stim zero + Resp zero + Var zero | total stillness; no energy, no change |
| Stress | Stim high + Res depleting + Damp overloaded | pushing against a constraint while running down |
| Flow **[dup]** | Stim optimal + Resp matched + Coup unity | input matches capacity; no friction, no lag, no waste — also Psychological T3 |
| Drift | Var slow + Coup low + Stim weak | slow undirected change; no attractor |
| Saturation | Stim excessive + Resp clipped | output at a ceiling; more input changes nothing |
| Habituation | Stim repeated + Var diminishing | the response weakens as the same thing keeps arriving |
| **Setpoint** **[dup: `Goal`, Desire]** | **Damp + Resp target** | **the reference a system compares its state against; the thing error is measured from. Cybernetic says it directly — `Goal seeking = Setpoint + Error + Action + Feedback`** |
| **Urgency** | **Res depleting + Thr approaching** | **time pressure shortening how much gets searched before acting. Not downstream of desire — `Frustration resp = Frust + Blockage`, so urgency needs wanting AND being unable** |
| **Momentum** | **Inr + Resp sustained** | **accumulated tendency to continue; distinct from inertia, which is resistance to starting** |
| **Latency wins** | **Lat + Thr passed** | **the outcome taken by the clock because nothing was chosen — not a branch selected, a default that fires on absence** |

**`Rest` is `bored()`, written by someone who had never seen it.** *Low stimulus, low variance,
high damping, nothing happens unless provoked.*

**And `Chaos` gets a better recipe here than the one composed for it in Part One.** `Entropy +
Variance` says *there is a lot of variation*. **`Sensitivity + Coupling + Variance` says why —
small differences amplify because the parts are joined** — which is the actual signal that a
question should be split rather than searched harder. **Use this recipe; the Part One entry
points here.**

**`Saturation` names a state the loop can reach and cannot report.** Output clipped, more input
changing nothing — **which is what a flat spread looks like from inside.**

## Tier 3 — compound states

| element | recipe | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| Criticality | Chaos + Equilibrium at the edge | poised between order and disorder; maximal information processing |
| Fatigue | Stress prolonged + Rec slow | depletion outpaces recovery; performance degrades |
| Resonance **[dup]** | Oscillation + Stim at frequency | amplitude grows because the input matches the natural rate — also Aesthetic T4, Human T4 |
| Avalanche | Chaos + Criticality + Sens | a small push cascades through the whole system |
| Metastability | Equilibrium unstable + Oscillation damped | near a stable point, easily kicked out of it |
| Burst | Saturation brief + Chaos local | a spike of activity, then quiet |
| Stalemate | Stress + Res zero + equal forces | two forces exactly balanced; nothing moves |
| Entrainment | Oscillation + Coup strong | two rhythms lock phase |
| Runaway | Chaos + Res unbounded + positive feedback | growth with nothing to stop it |
| **Hysteresis** | Lat + Thr differing by direction | the state depends on the path taken; the way up differs from the way down |
| **Ascent** | **Negative feedback + Momentum** | **error shrinking under correction; the trajectory that ends in a win** |
| **Descent** | **Positive feedback + Momentum** | **error growing because nothing corrects it; the trajectory that ends in a loss** |
| **Stagnation** | **Equilibrium + Res depleting** | **error neither shrinking nor growing while the budget runs down; the trajectory that ends in a draw** |
| **Reset** | **Deep rest + Setpoint restored** | **return to a known state deliberately, rather than being returned to one** |
| **Break habit** | **Dither + Witness stance + Damp** | **inject noise while watching, and dissipate the momentum — a brake, and `Damp` exists in Dynamic, Acoustic and Cybernetic already** |

**`Ascent`, `Descent` and `Stagnation` are one reading, not three states** — **the sign of the
feedback, read per step.** Negative shrinks the error, positive unchecked grows it, zero holds
it while the budget drains.

**Which makes the outcome legible before the outcome arrives.** *Am I ascending, descending or
stuck* is answerable at any step, **and all three components already existed separately.**

**And the two reset entries are the two ways a loop restarts.** `Reset` is *I learned something,
go again from a known state.* **`Break habit` is *I did not, so leave the groove and go back
further*** — and `Dither` is the right ingredient because small deliberate noise is what stops a
system sticking to a false equilibrium, while `Witness stance` is what notices it is stuck at
all.

**`Hysteresis` is the one to notice.** *The system's state depends on its history* — **and the
loop currently has no way to say that a slot got here by one route rather than another.**

**This is the bill.** The project already speaks this language, and the entries here are the

| element | recipe | what it does |

# EXTRA SCRUTINY — Thaumaturgical

**Twenty-six of fifty-four kept, all reframed.** The distinction that decides every one:

**The reasoning patterns are real. The causal claims are not.**

**Sympathetic magic is a documented human heuristic**, not a superstition people used to have —
*like affects like* and *once in contact, always connected* are how people actually reason, and
the literature on it is modern. **An agent that models other agents needs to know that they
reason this way.**

**And several entries name real operations under supernatural labels:**

| kept as | was | why it survives |
|---|---|---|
| **Similarity heuristic** | Sympathetic magic | treating resemblance as evidence of connection — which is also how analogy works, and how it fails |
| **Contagion heuristic** | Contagion | treating past contact as a persisting link; a real and measurable bias |
| **Ritual** | Ritual act | a fixed sequence repeated exactly because it worked once, **without knowing which part mattered** — superstitious conditioning, and a real agent failure |
| **Omen reading** | Divination | reading a pattern in noise as a prediction — **this is apophenia**, which the project already names |
| **Over-attribution of agency** | Animism | treating an inanimate thing as having a will; the Heider-Simmel prior firing where it should not |
| **Bounded region** | Circle | a marked area inside which the rules differ — real as a mechanic and real as a fact about spaces |
| **Frame-breaking surprise** | Ontological shock | **the strongest entry in the domain** — an event so far outside expectation that it challenges the frame rather than the prediction |
| **Carried token** | Talisman | an object held because it changes outcomes; the belief is real whether or not the mechanism is |

**`Frame-breaking surprise` is worth its own line.** *The moment an event violates what the
observer expects of physics* — **that is a residual large enough to indict the frame instead of
the bet**, and no other domain in three batches names it.

**What was cut: twenty-five entries**, all of them claims that an action on one thing
mechanically changes another through no channel — curses, blessings, pacts, summonings, voodoo
links, prophecy as fixed fate, the world soul, the sympathetic web.

**Not cut for being magic.** Cut because **the operation described does not exist**, which is a
different objection and the one that applies.

# EXTRA SCRUTINY — Noetic, revised

**First pass kept nine of forty-eight. That was the wrong test and the count is now thirty.**

## What the first pass got wrong

**It asked *is this an operation you can perform*.** Too narrow. **A state that biases every
subsequent appraisal has a causal role**, and a causal role is what matters — **not whether you
can do it on command.**

**And these are bidirectional.** They are a property of the system *and* the lossy channel
through which intent and residual become action. **Cutting them as reports rather than
operations cut the channel**, which is the part that does work.

## The three-channel argument, and it is structural

**Emotion is not produced by appraisal alone.** It arises from three things interacting:

**Internal feedback** — heart rate, hormones, tension, pain, interoception.
**External feedback** — sensory input, social cues, events, language, environment.
**Noetic processing** — attention, appraisal, memory, meaning-making, prediction.

**Which means Embodiment, Emotion and Noetic are one loop read at three places** — an
interoceptive residual, an environmental residual, and the appraisal that makes both mean
something.

**And that is the loop with a second channel.** *The body is a world the agent is also in*, and
it produces residuals the environment does not.

## Restored

| restored | why it comes back |
|---|---|
| **Ego suspension** | reversible, and it is what simulating an opponent requires: **hold their frame without being it** |
| **Grace** | *understanding arriving unearned* is **already in the priors** — insight arrives without a warmth gradient, Metcalfe & Wiebe. **The first pass cut something the corpus cites** |
| **Nondual awareness** | the subject/object split relaxing — **which is what a segmenter does when it stops separating figure from ground** |
| **Union** · **Awakening** | as **markers, not operations**: a reframe that does not revert |
| **Qualia** · **Subjectivity** | as the marker of **this state is being experienced rather than modelled about another** — the self/other line the mirror test needs |
| **Deep flow** · **Peak experience** · **Insight moment** · **Transformative insight** | states with downstream effects on what gets attempted next |
| **Ego death** · **Cosmic consciousness** · **Surrender win** · **Grace insight** | restored as extreme cases of the above, marked as such |
| **The self mirror** · **The witness state** · **The puzzle surrender** | see the miscibility ruling below |

## And the solver-dissolving entries come back, with a substrate condition

**These were cut on *a mirror cannot correct you*.** That was a collapse of a real distinction:

**Two fluids mix. Two rigid bodies cannot.** **Whether merging is available at all is a fact
about what the things are made of** — and the same is true of frames.

**So `the solver dissolves into the solution` holds where the frames are miscible and fails
where they are not**, and the condition is stateable rather than the claim being void.

**This produced three new Human tier-3 entries** — `Merge`, `Abut` and `Layer` — because the
list had only the fluid case. **`Amalgamate` assumed two things meeting become one**, with no
companion for *they meet and both persist*, and none at all for the layered case where **what is
underneath is still there, partly readable, and changes what the top does.**

## What survives from the first pass, unchanged

| kept as | was | why |
|---|---|---|
| **Sustained single focus** | Meditate | hold attention on one thing — trainable, and it is what a probe should be |
| **Incubation** | Contemplate | hold a problem without working on it. **`Eureka`'s missing half** |
| **Witness stance** | Witness | observe your own reasoning without being it — **the independence clause applied to yourself** |
| **Attentive action** | Mindful action | attention on the acting rather than the outcome |
| **Non-judgmental reframe** | Mindful frame | re-read without the valence already attached |
| **Solve by not forcing** | Contemplative solving | stop pushing on the framing and let another arrive |
| **Mindfulness** · **Awe** · **Silence** | — | Awe duplicates `The sublime` in Aesthetic |

## Still out — six of forty-eight

**The tier-7 monad and the entries whose whole content is that no distinction remains anywhere**
— *there is no one solving, nothing to solve, and no solved state.*

**Not for being esoteric.** **A frame with no ground has nothing to be corrected against**, and
that is different from two frames merging, which is now covered.

## Material

**Tier 1**

| element | atom | what it says |
|---|---|---|
| Str | Stress | Force per unit area applied to a material (pressure). The "load" on the system. |
| Strn | Strain | The deformation of a material relative to its original size (stretch, compress, twist). The "response." |
| Elas | Elasticity | Ability to deform under stress and *fully recover* when stress is removed. Linear region (Hooke's Law). |
| Plas | Plasticity | Ability to *permanently* deform when stress exceeds the elastic limit. No recovery. |
| Yiel | Yield Strength | The stress at which a material begins to deform plastically (the threshold of permanent damage). |
| Ult | Ultimate Tensile Strength | The maximum stress a material can withstand before it begins to neck/rupture. The peak. |
| Frac | Fracture Toughness | Resistance to crack propagation. High = tough (rubber). Low = brittle (glass). |
| Fati | Fatigue | Progressive weakening under cyclic loading (repeated stress). Cumulative damage over time. |
| Cre | Creep | Time-dependent, slow, permanent deformation under constant stress (like a glacier flowing). |
| Hard | Hardness | Resistance to localized surface indentation or scratching. |

**Tier 2**

| element | recipe | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| Hookes law | Str + Strn + Elas | Stress is proportional to strain (F = kx). The spring constant (k) defines stiffness. Predictable, reversible deformation. |
| Yield | Str > Yiel + Plas | Stress exceeds yield strength. The material bends permanently. It does not spring back. Shape changes forever. |
| Rupture | Str > Ult + Frac | Stress exceeds ultimate tensile strength. The material breaks completely. Separation into two pieces. |
| Brittle failure | Frac (low) + Str (sudden) | A material with low fracture toughness shatters instantly with no plastic deformation (glass, ceramic). |
| Ductile failure | Frac (high) + Plas (extensive) | A material stretches, necks, and then tears slowly (metal, chewing gum). Warning signs before break. |
| Fatigue cycle | Str (cyclic) + Fati + Time | Repeated stress below the yield point gradually weakens the material. After N cycles, it breaks without warning. |
| Creep deformation | Str (constant) + Cre + Time | Under sustained load, the material slowly flows. Over many frames, a shelf warps or a rope stretches. |
| Hardness test | Hard + Indent | A hard object (e.g., diamond) scores a softer material. Used to compare material toughness. |

**Tier 3**

| element | recipe | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| Stress strain curve | HOOKES_LAW + YIELD + RUPTURE | The full lifecycle of a material under load: elastic region → yield point → plastic region → ultimate strength → fracture. A visual graph of the material's "personality." |
| Composite | Brittle (hard) + Ductile (tough) | Combine two materials (e.g., fiberglass, reinforced concrete). The composite has the hardness of one and the toughness of the other. Resists cracks. |
| Anneal | Plas (reset) + Heat (Thermo) | Heat the material to soften it, then cool slowly. Removes internal stresses, resets fatigue damage, makes it more ductile. |
| Quench | Hard (increase) + Heat (rapid cool) | Heat then rapidly cool (water/oil). Hardens the material but makes it more brittle. Trade-off. |
| Work harden | Plas (repeated) + Hard (increase) | Repeated plastic deformation (hammering) increases hardness but decreases ductility. The material becomes stiffer until it cracks. |
| Residual stress | Str (trapped) + Elas (unrecovered) | Stress locked inside the material after uneven cooling or plastic deformation. It can suddenly release (a spring that "snaps"). |
| Viscelasticity | Elas + Cre + Time | A material that behaves like both a spring (elastic) and a dashpot (viscous). Memory foam, rubber bands at high speed. |

**Tier 4**

| element | recipe | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| Crack propagation | Frac (low) + Str (focused) | A microscopic flaw (crack) concentrates stress at its tip. The crack grows uncontrollably if the material is brittle. |
| Stress concentration | Geometry (sharp corner) + Str (amplified) | A sharp corner, notch, or hole multiplies the local stress. A 1x force becomes a 10x force at a crack tip. |
| Load path | Continuity (H) + Str + Structural_Members | Forces travel through a structure like water through a pipe. If the path is interrupted, stress redistributes and may overload a weaker member. |
| Buckling | Compressive_Str + Slenderness | A long, thin column (pencil) under compression suddenly bows sideways. It fails not by crushing, but by geometric instability. |
| Shear failure | Str (lateral) + Bond (Chemical) | Forces slide one layer of material against another. Rivets snap, glue peels, beams twist. |
| Impact damage | Str (sudden, high) + Frac + Plas (local) | A sudden hammer blow. If below yield, it dents (plastic). If above, it shatters (fracture). Energy is absorbed by deformation or crack creation. |
| Weld zone | Composite + Heat + Diffusion | Two separate pieces joined by melting and mixing. The weld zone has different properties—often weaker and more brittle. |

**Tier 5**

| element | recipe | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| Corrosion | Chemical (Oxidation) + Str (stress) | Chemical attack (rust) eats away the surface. Combined with stress, it leads to stress-corrosion cracking—premature failure at low loads. |
| Hydrogen embrittlement | Chemical (H₂) + Frac (lowered) | Small hydrogen atoms diffuse into the metal lattice, making it extremely brittle. A strong metal suddenly turns to glass. |
| Fatigue limit | Fati + Str (threshold) | Below a certain stress amplitude, some materials (steel) have an *infinite* fatigue life. Above it, they will eventually break after a predictable number of cycles. |
| Neutron irradiation | Quantum (radiation) + Hard (increase) | Exposure to radiation embrittles materials. They become hard and brittle over time (nuclear reactor pressure vessels). |
| Bauschinger effect | Plas (forward) + Yiel (reverse lower) | After plastic deformation in one direction, the yield strength in the opposite direction is reduced. Hysteresis in material memory. |
| Thermal fatigue | Thermo (expansion/contraction) + Fati | Repeated heating and cooling cause differential expansion. Cracks form due to thermal cycling. |
| Decohesion | Bond (Chemical) + Str (tension) | Molecules are pulled apart at the atomic level. The material separates along grain boundaries. The ultimate limit of all materials. |

**Tier 6**

| element | recipe | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| Damage mechanics | Crack_Propagation + Fati + Plas | A unified framework for predicting the life of a structure under real-world loads. Accumulated damage D = Σ(nᵢ / Nᵢ). When D = 1, failure occurs (Miner's rule). |
| Fracture mechanics | Str (at crack tip) + Frac + Critical | The stress intensity factor (K) at a crack tip. If K exceeds Kᵢc (critical fracture toughness), the crack runs and the part fails. This gives exact failure thresholds. |
| Perfect elasticity | Elas (infinite) + Fati (zero) | An ideal material that never yields, never fatigues, and always returns to its original shape. Unattainable in reality—but a useful theoretical maximum. |
| Perfect plasticity | Plas (infinite) + Hard (zero) | A material that deforms without any resistance once yield is exceeded. It flows like honey. |
| Metallurgical memory | Plas + Cre + Time | The material "remembers" its deformation history. The internal dislocation network is a physical record of stress events. |
| Granular structure | Hard + Frac + Boundary | The internal grain structure of polycrystalline materials. Grain boundaries are strong (resist slip) but can also be sites of weakness (intergranular fracture). |

**Every one carries a `[dup]` tag and a pointer.** Later categorisation will need to distinguish
them, and a merged entry cannot be un-merged once the other reading is gone.

**Ten entries were restored by ruling after the first pass**, with recipes recomposed from
what survived: `Movement`, `Chaos`, `Amalgamate`, `Separate`, `Erase`, `Construct`, `Phantom`,
`Conflate`, `Negotiate`, `Dissimulate`. **`Confabulate` was already at tier 4 and stayed there.**

**And `Misdirect` absorbed what `Ontological lie` was reaching for** — it was already at tier 3
and needed no new entry, only the note that it covers the case.

# What still has to be decided

**None of this is loaded.** It is a candidate list for the visible set, and three things are
open:

**Whether the non-Human tier 1 atoms carry provenance.** The Human ones cite developmental
evidence. Fluid, mechanical and heat are folk-level and a body meets all of them — **but no
citation exists**, so they would enter as `unattributed` rather than `derived` unless someone
supplies one.

**Whether tier 5 and 6 belong in the visible set at all**, given §12.3 says the agent must reach
for the higher compositions because reaching is the evidence. **A visible entry is not a held
one, so this is probably fine — but it is a ruling, not a fact.**

**And whether the recipes are checked or trusted.** Tier 2 was verified against the priors by
hand for four entries. **The rest are the original generator's, and a recipe that does not
entail its element is a plausible-looking wrong answer** — which is the class this project
treats most carefully.
