## How to treat these files

These are not an agent specification and not a picture book. They are the compact statement of a
**recursive, domain-agnostic, self-consistent metatheory that functions as a barrier theorem and
applies to all known systems and entities on every level (stratum)**. Every figure below is quoted
verbatim from the SVG; the quotes are the authority, not this summary.

### What each adjective means, from the figures

- **Recursive.** The same rules apply at every stratum, so a composed result becomes an operand at
  the next one. Fig 12: *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."*
  *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact
  about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom."* Fig 4 adds the other end:
  *"there is no top and no infinite regress."* So there is no smallest and no largest level — the
  theory has no privileged stratum.

- **Domain-agnostic.** What changes between domains and levels is only the cast, never the rules.
  Fig 4: *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule
  for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the
  currency and the anchor change."* Fig 10: *"Different actors, a different currency, a different
  ground, and a lossy transform between. That is why there is no top rung and no regress: a
  generator crosses, an instance does not."* This is why the same thirteen figures govern a board
  game, an organism, a market, or a physical theory: name the actors, the currency, the anchor,
  then apply the loop.

- **Self-consistent.** The figures constrain each other; a reading that makes one contradict
  another has misidentified its domain, not found a flaw. They are one set applied at once, not
  thirteen independent tips. Where two figures seem to strain, re-census the actors/currency/
  anchor before changing the reading (see the derive-first procedure in `.claude/skills/`).

- **Barrier theorem / metalaw.** The framework includes and excludes categorically at every level,
  so it narrows the shape of an answer without needing the answer in advance. Fig 6 names the one
  operator that changes the wall: *"the only operator that moves the wall"* (import). Fig 8: *"no
  search, however long, adds an atom"* — closure is idempotent, so search inside a frame cannot
  manufacture what the frame lacks. Fig 9 separates the verdict from the budget: *"the verdict: is
  φ* in here at all?"* reported as *unreachable*, not silence. A barrier theorem tells you what
  cannot be composed from what you hold, and what would have to be imported — that is its power.

- **All systems, all levels.** Fig 13 gives the minimum set that any such system needs: *"No system
  functions without any of them, none is built from the others, and everything else is a
  composition across the two kinds."* The six are two kinds in lockstep — *"the two kinds are in
  lockstep, a crossing is a change of role rather than a step upward, and no member of the pair is
  senior to the other"* — and *"composition across the two kinds, never within one."* States
  (space, time, energy) and structures (gradient, network, spectrum) alternate; any system at any
  stratum that composes anything is doing this. The Operators and Symbols tables are the notation
  for it.

- **The ground, not agreement, decides.** Fig 11: *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence
  is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent;
  selection ran."* and *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much
  it improves."* Fig 10: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does."* A frame can be perfectly
  self-consistent and still wrong; only the immovable ground settles it. This is what makes the
  theory falsifiable rather than a closed belief system.

### This is not a Theory of Everything — and that is the point

Theories of Everything cannot exist. A ToE would have to be the **final room**, a frame with no
outside left to import from — but the figures forbid that room. Fig 7 is titled *"the limit: no
last room"*: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not."* Handing a
question along a chain of agreeing frames never adds evidence. Fig 8: closure is idempotent, so no
amount of search inside a closed set adds an atom. Fig 12: *"there is no bottom"*; Fig 4: *"there
is no top."* A terminal, all-explaining frame would require both a bottom and a top, and the theory
rules both out definitionally.

So this framework is **not** the final answer and makes no claim to be. It is the next best thing:
a **generator of the shape of an answer at any level**, applied recursively until the residual
stops registering against the ground. It can say what is reachable from what you hold, what is
unreachable and therefore owed, and what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a
name) — then it stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records
the lineage so the stop is honest. Indiscernibility is not identity; the record of the route is
what keeps the result checkable. See `THE_FORMULA.md` for the step-by-step and
`docs/05-RESEARCH/60-WATSON_2026-11-22_FIGURES_AS_METALAWS.md` for a worked statement of the
metalaw reading.

### How to apply them (short form)

1. State the question in effect terms; name the actors, currency and anchor (Fig 4/10).
2. Read the figures that bear — quote them from these SVGs, not from a summary.
3. Derive the answer before concluding; a figure that strains was aimed at another quantity.
4. Check it against the ground (a measurement, a board, an experiment) — agreement between seats
   is one evidence pool, not a witness (Fig 2).
5. Keep the lineage: record what was unreachable, what was imported, and what would reverse it.

Cite a figure as `Fig N :line` against `docs/01-FOUNDATIONS/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`.
